# App precedents and developer experience: step-goal-reached notifications on a locked iPhone

Research date: 2026-10-19. Source dates are given where the page shows one; "undated" means the page shows no date. Many forum pages show only month and year. Unless a line says it was fetched, the detail comes from a search-result summary rather than a full-page read.

## Q1. Do shipping step and fitness apps send "goal reached" notifications while the iPhone is locked, and how delayed are they?

### Takeaway
The one app that states its notification timing in writing is StepsApp, which says goal alerts can arrive up to about an hour late because iOS allows only one background update per hour. No other app I checked (Pedometer++, Pacer, Stepz, Sweatcoin, Fitbit, Apple Fitness) documents a goal notification on a locked iPhone, let alone a fast one. I found no numeric delay measurements from users.

### Cited Findings
- **StepsApp (documented by the vendor, undated page, footer "© 2015-2026"):** "Once the notifications are on, there could be a delay when the goal is reached, because Apple only allows an update once every hour in the background." To reduce the delay, the page tells users to turn off power-saving mode and enable "Background Update" for the app. — [StepsApp Support: How do I set up the notifications?](https://steps.app/support/pedometer/ios/troubleshooting/how-do-i-set-up-the-notifications) (fetched)
- **StepsApp's Fitbit-sync app (same vendor, undated):** "Apple Health allows updates in background only once every hour. For this reason, sometimes, there might be a delay in the app." — [StepsApp Support: How often does the app update itself?](https://steps.app/support/fitbit-sync/ios/purchase/how-often-does-the-app-update-itself) (fetched)
- **Pedometer++ (David Smith / Cross Forward Consulting):** The current FAQ has no entry on notifications, goal alerts, background updates, Low Power Mode or location. It says the app "merges step data from Apple Health with information using its own algorithm" and that "Your iPhone is always collecting step counts for the Health app." — [Pedometer++ FAQ](https://pedometer.app/faq) (fetched; copyright 2026)
- **Pedometer++ v8 (blog post, 2026-04-28):** The redesigned in-app display "animates as you move and celebrates when you reach your goal." The post does not mention push notifications, background updates or Live Activities. — [David Smith: Pedometer++ v8](https://www.david-smith.org/blog/2026/04/28/pedometer-v8/) (fetched)
- **Pedometer++:** The App Store listing mentions Lock Screen and Home Screen widgets that show daily progress (search summary). I found no claim there about goal notifications. — [Pedometer++ on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pedometer/id712286167)
- **Pedometer++ on Apple Watch (2015, old):** Complications update "throughout the day (typically every 20-30 minutes)" to show progress toward the goal (search summary). — [David Smith: Pedometer++ 2.3](https://www.david-smith.org/blog/2015/12/03/pedometer-plus-plus-2-dot-3-embracing-the-apple-watch/)
- **Pacer:** The support article on missing notifications blames OS-level permission, Focus or Do Not Disturb, and iOS Notification Summary batching (search summary). It gives no timing for goal alerts. — [Pacer: I'm Not Receiving Notifications, Quick Fix Guide](https://support.mypacer.com/hc/en-us/articles/42241321915405-I-m-Not-Receiving-Notifications-Quick-Fix-Guide)
- **Apple Watch has no built-in step-count alert (user anecdote, about 2022):** A user asked how to be alerted at 10,000 steps. Replies pointed to third-party apps such as StepsApp rather than a built-in Apple alert. — [Apple Community thread 253925340](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/253925340)
- **Apple Fitness on iPhone without a Watch (since iOS 16):** The app tracks a Move goal from iPhone motion data, and setup asks for notification permission. I found no source describing a "Move goal reached" notification on an iPhone with no Watch. — [iPhone Life: Fitness app without Apple Watch](https://iphonelife.com/content/how-to-use-apple-fitness-app-ios-16); [iPhone in Canada: iOS 16 Fitness for all iPhones](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2022/06/06/ios-16-to-bring-the-fitness-app-to-all-iphones-no-apple-watch-needed/)
- **Fitbit:** Fitbit said goal "celebrations" were coming back to its app, reaching iOS in early January 2024. — [9to5Google, 2023-11-14](https://9to5google.com/2023/11/14/fitbit-steps-streak/). An iPhone user reports celebrations not appearing, with no accepted fix (search summary). — [Fitbit Community: No notification celebration](https://community.fitbit.com/t5/iOS-App/No-notification-celebration/td-p/1630202). Neither source says whether an iPhone-only user (no Fitbit device) gets a goal notification while the phone is locked.
- **Smaller apps (developer claims only):** "Steps Goal" lists a goal-reached notification and says it reads motion-sensor data. — [Steps Goal on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/es/app/id1549464228). "StepTracker" says it stays current "whether you have the app open or not," with no timing given. — [StepTracker on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1663569893)

### Inferences
- A well-known, actively maintained step app (StepsApp) tells its users in writing that goal notifications can be up to about an hour late. This suggests that well-known iPhone-only apps do not deliver near-instant goal alerts on a locked phone through supported mechanisms.
- Pedometer++ is the best-known indie pedometer, and its documentation does not advertise goal notifications at all. Its goal feedback appears to be in-app or on widgets and complications, which matches the platform limits.

### Gaps
- I found no measured or user-reported delay times (for example "arrived 40 minutes later") for Pedometer++, Pacer, Stepz, Sweatcoin, Google Fit, Fitbit, Strava or WeightWatchers. Searches of Reddit and App Store reviews returned nothing usable.
- I found nothing on goal notifications from Strava (when not recording), WeightWatchers, Google Fit for iOS, or ViewRanger-style apps.
- I could not confirm whether Apple's own Fitness app on an iPhone with no Watch sends a goal-reached notification, or how promptly.

## Q2. What mechanisms do these apps disclose or appear to use, and which ones request "Always" location?

### Takeaway
Pedometers that run only on the iPhone count steps with the motion coprocessor through Core Motion or Apple Health. Apps that claim continuous background tracking (Sweatcoin, the "Count steps" app) rely on background location, and some apps make location optional for GPS distance. No app I found discloses using silent push or a server to time goal notifications. I found no record of how App Review treated a step app that asks for Always location.

### Cited Findings
- **Pedometer++** gets steps through Apple Health, which is "always collecting step counts," and merges that data with its own algorithm. — [Pedometer++ FAQ](https://pedometer.app/faq) (fetched). An older review (2014) says Health pulls step data directly from the motion coprocessor (search summary). — [TidBITS 2014](https://tidbits.com/2014/10/24/funbits-pedometer-tracks-your-steps-on-the-cheap/)
- **StepsApp** attributes its delay to Apple's background update limit, which points to HealthKit background delivery or background refresh. — [StepsApp Support](https://steps.app/support/pedometer/ios/troubleshooting/how-do-i-set-up-the-notifications) (fetched)
- **Stepz** says it uses the motion coprocessor, which collects data automatically in the background (search summary). — [Stepz on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/stepz-step-counter-tracker/id839671656)
- **Pacer** counts steps with the motion coprocessor and asks for Motion & Fitness access. Its 2021 setup guide also includes a separate location prompt. — [UTA Pacer iPhone instructions (2021)](https://seniorfitstudy.uta.edu/files/2021/11/pacer_app_instruction_iPhone.pdf). The App Store listing calls location optional, for more accurate GPS tracking, and warns that continued background GPS use "may dramatically decrease battery life" (search summary). — [Pacer on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/pacer-pedometer-step-tracker/id600446812?l=en-US)
- **Sweatcoin:** The help center says the app tracks steps and geolocation in the background, and that turning off Location Services stops coin generation. Its troubleshooting asks users to enable Background App Refresh, and with Low Power Mode on, to open the app at least every 12 hours (search summary). — [Sweatcoin Help: How to stop Sweatcoin from running](https://help.sweatco.in/hc/it/articles/360012677092-How-to-stop-Sweatcoin-from-running); [Sweatcoin Help: Mobile data](https://help.sweatco.in/hc/it/articles/360012674672-Do-I-need-to-have-mobile-data-enabled-for-the-app-to-work). The press has criticised the app's battery drain. — [Yorkshire Post](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/read-this/sweatcoin-app-promises-money-for-exercise-but-critics-warn-itll-drain-your-battery-50346) (headline only)
- **"Count steps – Step counter app"** states that it needs location access to track distance in the background, and tells users to enable Location Services if counts stop updating in the background (search summary). — [Count steps on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/count-steps-step-counter-app/id1369581592)
- **"Pacer Volume"** (a different developer, not Pacer Health) requires Always location "to continually gauge your speed even when the app is in the background" (search summary). — [Pacer Volume on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6471933216)
- **Fitbit:** Fitbit says its app must run in the background for notifications and syncing, and that otherwise phone notifications may be delayed or missing. Its setup steps are for Android (search summary). — [Fitbit Community: App notifications](https://community.fitbit.com/t5/Android-App/App-notifications/td-p/3730087)

### Inferences
- Two patterns appear in practice. (a) Motion coprocessor or HealthKit data, refreshed on iOS's hourly background schedule (StepsApp, probably Pedometer++, Stepz and Pacer by default). (b) Continuous background location, which keeps the app alive and makes timely local notifications possible, justified by a user-facing location or distance feature (Sweatcoin, Count steps, Pacer's optional GPS). Pattern (b) costs battery and needs Always or ongoing location permission.
- The location-based apps tie location to a location feature (distance, speed, verification) rather than presenting it as a keep-alive for step alerts. That framing is probably what keeps them inside the App Review guidelines (see Q3).

### Gaps
- None of the apps says outright which mechanism triggers its goal notification. The mechanisms above are inferred from support pages and listing text.
- I found no public account of App Review's response to Sweatcoin's, Pacer's or Count steps' location use.
- I could not confirm whether any of these apps uses an Apple Watch companion or silent push to time goal notifications.

## Q3. What do developers report trying, and what failed or was rejected?

### Takeaway
Apple's documentation states, and Apple DTS confirmed in 2026, that HealthKit `stepCount` background delivery on iOS is capped at once per hour even with `.immediate`. Developers also report gaps of several hours: when the battery is low or was recently drained, when the app was force-quit, and outside Xcode, where tests look better than reality. Silent push is throttled to roughly two or three per hour on a budget shared across the device. Core Motion live updates stop in the background unless the app also runs continuous background location, and a declared background-location mode without a matching user feature is a common reason for rejection under guideline 2.5.4. I found no source on using significant location change as a wake-up for step alerts.

### Cited Findings

**HealthKit background delivery: documented limits**
- From Apple's documentation (2026): "Some sample types have a maximum frequency of `HKUpdateFrequency.hourly`. The system enforces this frequency transparently. For example, on iOS, `stepCount` samples have an hourly maximum frequency." On watchOS, background updates share a budget with background refresh tasks: "four updates (or background app refresh tasks) an hour, as long as it has a complication on the active watch face." If the app fails to call the completion handler three times, "HealthKit assumes your app can't receive data and stops sending background updates." — [Apple: enableBackgroundDelivery(for:frequency:withCompletion:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/enablebackgrounddelivery(for:frequency:withcompletion:)) (fetched)

**HealthKit background delivery: developer reports (forums)**
- **April 2024 (developer report):** Step-count background delivery fired when the app was run from Xcode but not when it was launched on the device. The developer's own answer: "While updates from health data are immediate in sandbox, you receive updates at hourly rate in production." — [Apple Developer Forums 750828](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/750828) (fetched)
- **April 2026 (developer report plus DTS answer):** With `.immediate`, updates sometimes arrived promptly and sometimes not at all. With `.hourly`, they were "delayed, batched, or skipped." A `BGAppRefreshTask` with `earliestBeginDate` of about one hour was sometimes delayed by several hours. — [Apple Developer Forums 823699](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/823699) (fetched; DTS reply quoted in Q4)
- **January 2026 (developer report plus DTS partial reproduction):** Hourly step monitoring on an iPhone 17 with iOS 26.2. Callbacks stopped 3-4 hours after unplugging and resumed immediately on charging. After the battery once dropped below 10%, delivery did not return even at 100% charge with Low Power Mode off. There is no delivery at all with Low Power Mode on or Background App Refresh off. Apple DTS: "That background delivery was ever disabled by the system due to the low battery level is likely the culprit. I am not quite sure if that is as-designed though." Filed as FB21635120. — [Apple Developer Forums 812200](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/812200) (fetched)
- **October 2025 (developer report plus Apple answer):** A React Native fitness app used `.immediate` step-count delivery, and it stopped after the user force-quit the app. Apple: force quit "sets a flag that prevents the app from being launched in the background," and "there's no documented way for your app to override the user's choice." — [Apple Developer Forums 803365](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/803365) (fetched)
- **September 2024 (developer report):** HealthKit observer, background delivery and a background upload of steps worked in the foreground but never in the background. Expo Background Fetch and silent push uploads were "too irregular." — [Apple Developer Forums 763701](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/763701) (fetched)
- **Apple Watch (developer report):** Some devices were throttled to about hourly, while others woke about every 8-16 minutes with identical configuration (search summary). — [Apple Developer Forums 814914](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/814914)
- **Vendor documentation (secondary):** Junction says step count has an hourly maximum, subject to throttling, and that actual delivery "can fluctuate from hourly, to once a day, or when a certain opportunity arises" (search summary). — [Junction: Apple HealthKit guide](https://docs.junction.com/wearables/guides/apple-healthkit). Sahha makes the same hourly-cap point (search summary). — [Sahha: Why wearable data arrives late](https://sahha.ai/blog/why-wearable-data-is-late/)

**Silent push (server-driven wake-up)**
- **Apple engineer, May 2026, accepted answer:** The documentation line ("don't try to send more than two or three per hour") "is still valid." Testing through Xcode removes the throttle, and so does having the app in the foreground. The throttle "is based on device wide budgets for silent notifications." Purely silent pushes must use priority 5 and are "throttled first at APNs, and then on the device." A push with visible alert content can use priority 10: the alert is shown and not throttled at APNs, but "passing the content to your app will still be throttled." — [Apple Developer Forums 827460](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/827460) (fetched)
- The throttle is disabled when a debugger is attached, so test results are a best case (search summary). — [Apple Developer Forums 760244](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/760244)
- A developer quoting Apple says throttling got stricter in iOS 15 and relaxed somewhat in iOS 15.4 (search summary; secondhand). — [Apple Developer Forums profile comments](https://origin-devforums.apple.com/forums/profile/TheRealStarone/comments)

**Core Motion plus background location keep-alive**
- **Japanese developer blog, 2025-08-31:** `CMPedometer` step updates, and the local notifications built on them, worked in the background only while the app was attached to Xcode. Once detached, background operation stopped. The author got periodic background notifications by adding `startUpdatingLocation()`, `allowsBackgroundLocationUpdates = true`, `pausesLocationUpdatesAutomatically = false` and `UIBackgroundModes: location`. With "While Using" permission the status bar shows the blue location indicator; with "Always" it does not. The post does not discuss App Review. — [DevelopersIO (Classmethod): CMPedometerで歩数取得](https://dev.classmethod.jp/articles/ios-cmpedometer-step-count/) (fetched)

**App Review guideline 2.5.4 (background modes)**
- In one rejection thread, the reviewer objected to an app using the location background mode for employee tracking, and the developer reported that appeals failed (search summary). — [Apple Developer Forums 118275](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/118275)
- **2018:** The reviewer asked, "What features or functionality in the app require Background Location to be enabled?" The developer had removed location code and was still rejected. Info.plist location keys or the Background Modes capability were suspected. No resolution was posted. — [Apple Developer Forums 108641](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/108641) (fetched)
- A 2.5.4 rejection over `UIBackgroundModes` (search summary). — [Apple Developer Forums 771202](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/771202)
- `expo-location` adds `UIBackgroundModes: location` even when the app only needs geofencing, which "gets the app rejected under App Store guideline 2.5.4" (search summary). — [expo/expo issue 50194](https://github.com/expo/expo/issues/50194)

### Inferences
- In practice, HealthKit-based step-goal notifications on a locked iPhone arrive within about an hour at best, and can be hours late or never arrive (low battery, Low Power Mode, force quit, Background App Refresh off). Tests run from Xcode overstate how quickly they arrive.
- Silent push cannot make goal alerts faster. It is throttled to roughly the same hourly scale, and the server has no way to know the user's step count until the device uploads it.
- The only technique I found that developers report for sub-hourly local step notifications is continuous background location as a keep-alive. That is the pattern guideline 2.5.4 rejections target when the app has no genuine location feature.

### Gaps
- I found no developer post measuring significant-location-change monitoring as a wake-up for step-goal checks, and no report of App Review's response to that use.
- I found no App Review rejection that names a step or pedometer app specifically. The 2.5.4 cases above involve other app types or configuration mistakes.
- I found no Stack Overflow measurement of locked-phone `stepCount` delivery intervals. The evidence is the Apple Developer Forums threads above.
- I could not verify whether `CMPedometer.queryPedometerData` returns current data during a background launch while the device is locked.

## Q4. What have Apple engineers (DTS) said about notifying a user soon after a step goal is reached?

### Takeaway
I found no Apple statement endorsing a fast, locked-phone step-goal notification on iPhone. The only recommendation is HealthKit background delivery with an observer query plus a statistics query (2020). In 2024-2026 DTS added the limits: `stepCount` is at most hourly even with `.immediate`, delivery is not guaranteed, nothing changes in HealthKit while the iPhone is locked, and nothing overrides a force quit.

### Cited Findings
- **Apple Frameworks Engineer, June 2020,** on firing notifications at step-goal thresholds: "You'll want to look into Background Delivery ... This will allow HealthKit to launch your application in the background when data that you're interested in is saved. You'll use that in combination with HKObserverQuery and, usually, HKAnchoredObjectQuery or HKStatisticsQuery/HKStatisticsCollectionQuery." The engineer also recommends statistics queries for "correct de-duplication between samples ... from different devices." — [Apple Developer Forums 134282](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/134282) (fetched)
- **Quinn "The Eskimo!" (DTS), July 2020,** on a step-goal notification app whose background notifications were not firing: he gave no technical guidance and suggested opening a DTS tech support incident. — [Apple Developer Forums 654269](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/654269) (fetched)
- **Ziqiao Chen (DTS), September 2024:** "when your iPhone is locked, the system can't write any data to the HealthKit store, and so no change will happen." He adds that HealthKit background updates are budgeted: "We don't have a similar documentation for iOS, but if the observed Health data type changes very frequently, I won't expect that every change triggers a background update." — [Apple Developer Forums 763701](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/763701) (fetched)
- **Ziqiao Chen (DTS), April 2026:** "`stepCount` samples have an hourly maximum frequency in iOS, and so the `updateHandler` will be triggered at most once per hour, even you use `enableBackgroundDelivery` + `.immediate`." Also: "the delivery frequency is not guaranteed. The system tries to honor the specified frequency, but may not achieve that due to the app's background execution time budget and other undocumented factors, and there is no API to change the behavior." And on BGTaskScheduler: "The detailed mechanism the system uses to schedule background tasks is undocumented." — [Apple Developer Forums 823699](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/823699) (fetched)
- **Ziqiao Chen (DTS), January 2026:** "the system may decide to not deliver an update if the battery level is low, even the device isn't in the low battery mode." — [Apple Developer Forums 812200](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/812200) (fetched)
- **Apple engineer, October 2025, on force quit:** Apple advises teaching users that force quit stops data collection. The server can send a visible push when data looks stale, to prompt a relaunch. Alternatively, a Notification Service Extension runs when that push arrives "and try reading the data you need from the Health database," with the caveat of "limited memory, time, and access sandbox." — [Apple Developer Forums 803365](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/803365) (fetched)

### Inferences
- Apple's position, pieced together from these replies, is that HealthKit background delivery is the supported path, that step updates are at most hourly, and that there is no API for faster delivery. A notification "soon after" a goal is reached on a locked iPhone is therefore not a supported guarantee.
- The September 2024 DTS remark that HealthKit cannot change while the phone is locked suggests HealthKit-based goal detection may wait until the user unlocks the phone, even beyond the hourly cap. This is consistent with the forum OP's own premise ([thread 134282](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/134282), [thread 654269](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/654269)) that HealthKit cannot be queried while locked. Apple did not explicitly confirm that premise in those threads.
- Apple's suggested Notification Service Extension workaround needs a server push, and the extension reading Health data may hit the same locked-device restriction. Apple's "limited ... access" caveat points that way, but this is unverified.

### Gaps
- I found no DTS or Apple engineer statement that specifically recommends a design for "notify the user right after the daily step goal is reached" on iPhone. I also found no statement on whether Core Motion plus background location is acceptable for that purpose.
- I found no Apple documentation, in this pass, that states exactly when locked-device HealthKit writes are deferred and then flushed. The only claim is the DTS forum remark above.

# Passive background wake mechanisms on iPhone (iOS 16–26) for a step-goal notification while locked

Research date: 2026-10-19. Scope: ways the system can wake or relaunch an iPhone app without the user opening it or starting anything, so that it can read a step count and post a local notification within about 5 minutes of a goal being reached while the phone is locked in a pocket. Honoured does not otherwise need location.

Source conventions: "Documented" means Apple documentation, WWDC sessions, or replies by Apple DTS or App Review staff. "Anecdotal" means third-party developers or vendors. Apple documentation pages render through JavaScript, so their text was read from Apple's documentation JSON endpoint (`developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/<path>.json`). The cited URLs are the human-readable pages. The Apple-hosted WWDC20 session page redirected to the WWDC20 index during research, so its transcript was read from a mirror (nonstrict.eu), and both URLs are given.

## Silent/background push notifications (content-available)

### Takeaway
Silent pushes are best-effort, low priority, coalesced and rate-limited. Apple says "don't try to send more than two or three per hour". An Apple DTS engineer says to expect "1 or 2 push notifications per hour across all apps on a device", and possibly none. The system holds only the newest pending push, does not deliver to an app the user has force-quit, and applies Low Power Mode, the Background App Refresh switch and energy budgets. A server sending one every few minutes during a goal window would be throttled and coalesced. Apple's own best-case example shows about one launch every 15 minutes. This cannot reliably meet a 5-minute target.

### Cited Findings
- Apple: "The system treats background notifications as low priority: you can use them to refresh your app's content, but the system doesn't guarantee their delivery. In addition, the system may throttle the delivery of background notifications if the total number becomes excessive. The number of background notifications allowed by the system depends on current conditions, but don't try to send more than two or three per hour." — [Apple: Pushing background updates to your App](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/pushing-background-updates-to-your-app)
- Required headers: the `aps` dictionary contains only `content-available`. The request should set `apns-push-type: background` and `apns-priority: 5`. The app needs the "Remote notification" background mode. — [Apple: Pushing background updates to your App](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/pushing-background-updates-to-your-app)
- Holding and coalescing on the device: "When the system receives a new background notification, it discards the older notification and only holds the newest one. If something force quits or kills the app, the system discards the held notification. If the user launches the app, the system immediately delivers the held notification." — [Apple: Pushing background updates to your App](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/pushing-background-updates-to-your-app)
- Runtime per wake: "Your app has 30 seconds to perform any tasks and call the provided completion handler." — [Apple: Pushing background updates to your App](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/pushing-background-updates-to-your-app). The UIApplicationDelegate method says "up to 30 seconds of wall-clock time" and adds: "Apps that use significant amounts of power when processing remote notifications may not always be woken up early to process future notifications." — [Apple: application(_:didReceiveRemoteNotification:fetchCompletionHandler:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiapplicationdelegate/application(_:didreceiveremotenotification:fetchcompletionhandler:))
- Rate limit restated in a second Apple article: "If you send background pushes more frequently than three times per hour, the system imposes rate limitations." The same article says: "The system decides when to launch the app to download the content." — [Apple: Choosing background strategies for your app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/choosing-background-strategies-for-your-app)
- Apple DTS engineer Argun Tekant (Dec 2024): "notifications sent at low priority (priority 5) are throttled, regardless of payload. And background push notifications must always be sent at low priority. While you may expect up to several 1 or 2 push notifications per hour across all apps on a device, it is entirely possible and appropriate that you may receive none at all. Additionally, these notifications will not be delivered to the app at all if the user has not launched the app for a while." — [Apple Developer Forums thread 770864](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/770864)
- Same DTS reply: "The throttle is disabled if you run your app with a debugger attached… should only be considered a best-case scenario." Also: "apps should never be designed expecting that every background push notification will be received." — [Apple Developer Forums thread 770864](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/770864)
- Force quit, documented in three places:
  - "the system does not automatically launch your app if the user has force-quit it. In that situation, the user must relaunch your app or restart the device before the system attempts to launch your app automatically again." — [Apple: application(_:didReceiveRemoteNotification:fetchCompletionHandler:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiapplicationdelegate/application(_:didreceiveremotenotification:fetchcompletionhandler:))
  - DTS: "an app will not be woken in the background by a push notification if the app had previously been force-quit." — [Forums thread 770864](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/770864)
  - WWDC25: "when someone removes your app from the app switcher, the system respects their intent. The notifications are not delivered to your app until it's launched again." — [WWDC25 "Finish tasks in the background"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/227/)
- WWDC25 also calls background pushes discretionary: "they are always considered discretionary. They're also sent at a low priority and coalesced to minimize overhead and power cost." — [WWDC25 "Finish tasks in the background"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/227/)
- WWDC20 names seven factors that limit background runtime: "critically low battery, Low Power Mode, app usage, the App switcher, the Background App Refresh switch, system budgets, and rate-limiting". For background pushes: "The seven factors come into play for background pushes as well except for one: app usage." — [WWDC20 "Background execution demystified"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10063/) (transcript via [nonstrict.eu mirror](https://nonstrict.eu/wwdcindex/wwdc2020/10063/))
- WWDC20 on the Background App Refresh switch: "it applies to every mode we will discuss today" (background pushes included). On Low Power Mode: "given it is such a strong and direct signal, the system will take this as a cue to limit certain activity." On the App Switcher: "when the system determines which apps to run, it constrains to the set of apps that are still visible in the App Switcher." — [WWDC20 session 10063](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10063/) ([transcript mirror](https://nonstrict.eu/wwdcindex/wwdc2020/10063/))
- WWDC20's rate-limiting example: "Say in a given window, you send 14 pushes… The system delays the delivery of some pushes… So the 14 pushes we had result in seven launches… the time interval between runs was just 15 minutes." — [WWDC20 session 10063](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10063/) ([transcript mirror](https://nonstrict.eu/wwdcindex/wwdc2020/10063/))
- Low Power Mode turns off Background App Refresh: "Background App Refresh is disabled automatically when a device is operating in low-power mode. When this happens, the time available for performing background tasks is reduced to save power." — [Apple: UIApplication.backgroundRefreshStatus](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiapplication/backgroundrefreshstatus)
- Anecdotal: a developer asks whether silent pushes wake force-quit apps, which would contradict the documentation. This is a non-Apple reply. — [Forums thread 125789](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/125789)

### Inferences
- A server cannot push "every few minutes" in a goal window and expect a wake each time. Above about 3 per hour the pushes are rate-limited. The device keeps only the newest pending push, so a burst collapses into one delayed delivery. Apple's favourable example cadence (about 15 minutes between launches) is already 3 times the 5-minute target.
- Low Power Mode disables Background App Refresh, and WWDC20 says that switch "applies to every mode". In Low Power Mode, silent pushes should therefore be expected not to wake the app at all. This conclusion combines two Apple statements; Apple has not said it in one sentence.
- A silent push also needs the server to know when to send it, which means knowing that the goal is close. For steps that exist only on the phone, that is circular. It works only if step data reaches the server through another path, such as a wearable vendor's cloud API, which is outside this note's scope.
- A force-quit app gets no silent pushes until it is reopened. Many users swipe apps away habitually, so the mechanism fails silently for them.

### Gaps
- Apple publishes no exact numbers for the silent-push budget, the Low Power Mode behaviour, or how long "not launched for a while" is.
- No independent measurement of silent-push delivery latency on a locked, pocketed iPhone on iOS 26 was found.

## BGAppRefreshTask and BGProcessingTask

### Takeaway
Neither gives periodic or timely execution. App refresh tasks get about 30 seconds at times the system picks from usage predictions, budgets and switches. `earliestBeginDate` is only a lower bound. Processing tasks run only when the device is idle, typically overnight on a charger. Neither runs for an app the user has force-quit until it is relaunched. Neither can deliver a step notification within 5 minutes.

### Cited Findings
- `earliestBeginDate`: "Setting the property indicates that the background task shouldn't start any earlier than this date. However, the system doesn't guarantee launching the task at the specified date, but only that it won't begin sooner." — [Apple: BGTaskRequest.earliestBeginDate](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/bgtaskrequest/earliestbegindate)
- App refresh runtime: "The system decides the best time to launch your background task, and provides your app up to 30 seconds of background runtime." — [Apple: Choosing background strategies for your app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/choosing-background-strategies-for-your-app). It requires the `fetch` background mode. — [Apple: BGAppRefreshTask](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/bgapprefreshtask)
- Apple DTS (Quinn, "iOS Background Execution Limits", revised 2026-01-09): "if you expect that the app refresh mechanism will grant you background execution time, say, every 15 minutes, you'll be disappointed. In fact, there are common scenarios where it won't grant you _any_ background execution time at all!" The post lists "Running code periodically at a guaranteed interval" as something with no general-purpose mechanism, and answers "How do I run code in the background every 15 minutes?" with "You can't." — [Apple Developer Forums thread 685525](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/685525)
- Same post on force quit: when the user swipes an app away, "iOS also sets a flag that prevents the app from being launched in the background. That flag gets cleared when the user next launches the app manually… In some circumstances iOS will not honour this flag. The exact cases where this happens are not documented and have changed over time." — [Forums thread 685525](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/685525)
- WWDC19: app refresh gives "30 seconds of run time, each time it's launched… How often your app is launched and at what times depends on how the user has historically used your app," and "an app that isn't used as frequently may not launch as frequently as well." The example sets `earliestBeginDate` 15 minutes ahead to mimic the old minimum fetch interval. — [WWDC19 "Advances in App Background Execution"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/707/)
- WWDC20: refreshes are scheduled around predicted use, for example "in the morning, around lunchtime, and in the evening". Low Power Mode and critically low battery can cause "a large gap in launches". — [WWDC20 session 10063](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10063/) ([transcript mirror](https://nonstrict.eu/wwdcindex/wwdc2020/10063/))
- BGProcessingTask: "Processing tasks run only when the device is idle. The system terminates any background processing tasks running when the user starts using the device." It requires the `processing` background mode. — [Apple: BGProcessingTask](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/bgprocessingtask)
- WWDC20: for processing tasks "the system will wait to run you until the user is not actively using the device, such as when it's charging at night". As long as the user plugs in every day, the tasks "should be able to run daily". — [WWDC20 session 10063](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10063/) ([transcript mirror](https://nonstrict.eu/wwdcindex/wwdc2020/10063/))
- Low Power Mode disables Background App Refresh. — [Apple: backgroundRefreshStatus](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiapplication/backgroundrefreshstatus)
- iOS 26 adds `BGContinuedProcessingTask`, but it is for continuing work the user started in the foreground ("To continue user-visible work after the user has left your app"), so it is not passive. — [Forums thread 685525](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/685525); [WWDC25 session 227](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/227/)

### Inferences
- App refresh might sometimes happen to run shortly after a goal is reached, but Apple says this is not guaranteed and is driven by predicted usage. A pocketed, locked walk is not a predicted "app open" moment, so app refresh is useful only as an opportunistic catch-up.
- BGProcessingTask is unsuitable by design, because it requires an idle device.

### Gaps
- No credible measurements of real-world BGAppRefreshTask frequency on iOS 17–26 were found, only Apple's qualitative statements.

## Core Location wake mechanisms

### Takeaway
Core Location is the only family of documented passive mechanisms that can relaunch a terminated app in the background on movement:
- **Significant-change service:** events after moving 500 m or more, "not more frequently than once every five minutes", using cell and Wi-Fi.
- **Visits:** delivered late, by design.
- **Region monitoring (`CLMonitor`, legacy `CLRegion`):** fires on boundary crossings, at most 20 conditions.
- **Continuous background location (`allowsBackgroundLocationUpdates`, `CLBackgroundActivitySession`, `CLLocationUpdate.liveUpdates`):** keeps the app running, pauses automatically when stationary, and shows the location indicator for When-In-Use apps.

Relaunch after a user force quit is inconsistently documented. The archived guide says region monitoring and significant-change relaunch the app. WWDC23 says liveUpdates "can recover your app in most cases" after "user close". A single May 2026 report says the significant-change and region relaunch after a force quit stopped working on iOS 26.4.2. None of these mechanisms is triggered by steps. A walker produces location events only by covering distance, so indoor, treadmill and small-loop walking produce no wakes.

### Cited Findings
**Significant-change location service**
- "Apps can expect a notification as soon as the device moves 500 meters or more from its previous notification. It should not expect notifications more frequently than once every five minutes. If the device is able to retrieve data from the network, the location manager is much more likely to deliver notifications in a timely manner." — [Apple: startMonitoringSignificantLocationChanges()](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanager/startmonitoringsignificantlocationchanges())
- Relaunch: "If you start this service and your app is subsequently terminated, the system automatically relaunches the app into the background if a new event arrives… the options dictionary… contains the key [location]… Upon relaunch, you must still configure a location manager object and call this method to continue receiving location events." — [Apple: startMonitoringSignificantLocationChanges()](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanager/startmonitoringsignificantlocationchanges())
- Power: "This service uses the cellular and Wi-Fi radios (not GPS) to report only location changes that exceed a significantly large distance." — [Apple: Getting the current location of a device](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/getting-the-current-location-of-a-device)
- Archived Energy Efficiency Guide: "Register for Significant-Change Location Updates Only as a Last Resort… Region and visit monitoring are sufficient for most use cases and should always be considered before significant-change location updates. Significant-change location updates run continuously, around the clock, until you stop them, and can actually result in higher energy use if not employed effectively." It also says significant-change "require[s] an authorization status of kCLAuthorizationStatusAuthorizedAlways". — [Apple Energy Efficiency Guide for iOS Apps: Location Best Practices (archived)](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/Performance/Conceptual/EnergyGuide-iOS/LocationBestPractices.html)
- Apple suggests significant-change as the lighter alternative to the location background mode: "apps can use the significant-change location service to receive location events as an alternative to using the Location updates background mode." — [Apple: Configuring background execution modes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/configuring-background-execution-modes)

**Visit monitoring**
- "The Visits location service provides the most power-efficient way to get location data. The system monitors the places someone visits and the time they spend there, and delivers that data at a later time." — [Apple: Getting the current location of a device](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/getting-the-current-location-of-a-device)
- "If your app is terminated while this service is active, the system relaunches your app when new visit events are ready to be delivered." — [Apple: startMonitoringVisits()](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanager/startmonitoringvisits())

**Region / condition monitoring (CLMonitor on iOS 17+, CLRegion before it)**
- "In iOS, the system monitors regions and wakes up your app as needed when conditions change between satisfied and unsatisfied states… If an iOS app isn't running when a condition is satisfied, the system tries to launch it. When the app relaunches, recreate the monitor with the same identifier. Note that monitoring can only occur after the user unlocks the device after a reboot." — [Apple: Monitoring the user's proximity to geographic regions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/monitoring-the-user-s-proximity-to-geographic-regions)
- Limit: "Core Location prevents any single app from monitoring more than 20 conditions of any type simultaneously." — [same article](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/monitoring-the-user-s-proximity-to-geographic-regions). The legacy API says: "An app can register up to 20 regions at a time. In order to report region changes in a timely manner, the region monitoring service requires network connectivity." — [Apple: startMonitoring(for:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanager/startmonitoring(for:))
- `CLMonitor` monitors "events such as the entry to a specific geographic area or proximity to a beacon". — [Apple: CLMonitor](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/clmonitor-2r51v)
- A related system-run trigger, `UNLocationNotificationTrigger`, needs only When-In-Use permission "Because the system actually monitors the regions". It posts a pre-scheduled notification on entry or exit and does not let the app compute anything. — [Apple: UNLocationNotificationTrigger](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/unlocationnotificationtrigger)

**Continuous background location (iOS 16 legacy API and iOS 17+ API)**
- `allowsBackgroundLocationUpdates`: "When the value of this property is [true] and you start location updates while the app is in the foreground, Core Location configures the system to keep the app running to receive continuous background location updates, and arranges to show the background location indicator (blue bar or pill) if needed." It requires `UIBackgroundModes` to include `location`, and setting it without that key is "a fatal error". — [Apple: allowsBackgroundLocationUpdates](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanager/allowsbackgroundlocationupdates)
- Apple lists legitimate reasons for background updates, including to "Generate time-sensitive notifications or updates". It also says "Most apps need location data only while someone actively uses the app." — [Apple: Handling location updates in the background](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/handling-location-updates-in-the-background)
- `CLBackgroundActivitySession` (iOS 17+): "An object that manages a visual indicator that keeps your app in use in the background, allowing it to receive updates or events… allows a when-in-use authorized app to receive location updates or monitoring events." — [Apple: CLBackgroundActivitySession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/clbackgroundactivitysession-3mzv3)
- WWDC23: the session "uses the same indicator" (the blue background location indicator). "Just creating the session displays the indicator when your app is in the background." "Your app still needs to have location in its UIBackgroundModes array." "If you don't have an outstanding session, then you must start the new session from the foreground, but you can only rejoin an existing one from the the background." — [WWDC23 "Discover streamlined location updates"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10180/)
- WWDC23 on termination, including user close: the app can be terminated "due to app crash, or user close or system termination… Our API can recover your app in most cases, even when it's terminated and not running at all. We will recover your app as soon as location updates are available by launching it in the background". The app must recreate liveUpdates and the `CLBackgroundActivitySession` immediately on launch. — [WWDC23 session 10180](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10180/)
- WWDC23 on automatic pause: when the device is stationary for long enough, liveUpdates sends `isStationary = true` and pauses. Updates "automatically resume" when movement starts, and the app may be suspended during the pause and resumed when updates are available. — [WWDC23 session 10180](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10180/)
- WWDC24: `CLServiceSession` declares the authorization goal. Core Location "does not generate or deliver updates for which an app is not authorized, for example due to having .whenInUse authorization, and being backgrounded without a LiveActivity or CLBackgroundActivitySession in effect." With approximate location, updates come "only every 15 to 20 minutes" (`.accuracyLimited`). — [WWDC24 "What's new in location authorization"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10212/); [Apple: CLServiceSession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/clservicesession-pt7n)
- After termination, "you must recreate the [CLServiceSession] immediately upon launch in the background". — [Apple: Handling location updates in the background](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/handling-location-updates-in-the-background). SwiftUI apps need an `AppDelegate` so that events can resume "in the event of return from background, launch of the app, or relaunch after a crash". — [Apple: Supporting live updates in SwiftUI and Mac Catalyst apps](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/supporting-live-updates-in-swiftui-and-mac-catalyst-apps)
- Power-saving advice: for apps with in-use authorization, Apple suggests "disabling this property [pausesLocationUpdatesAutomatically] and changing location accuracy to kCLLocationAccuracyThreeKilometers when your app moves to the background. This allows your app to continue receiving location updates in a power-friendly manner." — [Apple: pausesLocationUpdatesAutomatically](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanager/pauseslocationupdatesautomatically)

**Force-quit relaunch behaviour, which conflicts across sources**
- Archived Location and Maps Programming Guide, as quoted in a forum post: "If your app is terminated either by a user or by the system, the system doesn't automatically restart your app when new location updates arrive… The only way to have your app relaunched automatically is to use region monitoring or the significant-change location service." — quoted in [Forums thread 701377](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/701377), citing the [archived Location Awareness guide](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/UserExperience/Conceptual/LocationAwarenessPG/CoreLocation/CoreLocation.html)
- The same thread (Feb 2022, non-Apple poster) reports no relaunch after a force quit for region monitoring or significant-change in their test. No Apple reply was captured. — [Forums thread 701377](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/701377)
- Single-developer report (May 2026, iOS 26.4.2, Always authorization, `UIBackgroundModes: location`, legacy significant-change plus `CLCircularRegion`): over "a 30km / 1-hour drive… the app is never relaunched… The same code on iOS 18 and earlier did relaunch the app from force-quit via SLC." There is no Apple reply. — [Forums thread 826964](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/826964)
- Quinn: the force-quit flag is sometimes not honoured, in cases that "are not documented and have changed over time". — [Forums thread 685525](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/685525)
- Vendor claim (Radar SDK): its SDK "is able to wake up and start collecting locations again if it receives certain types of location updates", including after a swipe-up kill. This requires background location permission and the location background mode. — [Radar FAQ](https://docs.radar.com/faqs.md)

**Runtime per location wake**
- Apple documents how a launch into the background happens (launch, `applicationDidEnterBackground`, event delivery, "The app may be suspended again"), but not a duration. — [Apple: About the background execution sequence](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/about-the-background-execution-sequence)

### Inferences
- **Significant-change timing.** At an assumed stride of 0.7–0.8 m, 500 m is roughly 625–715 steps; at about 5 km/h it takes about 6 minutes. So while walking outdoors in a straight line, significant-change wakes could come about every 6 minutes or more. Indoor, treadmill and small-loop walking produce essentially no events. Cell and Wi-Fi detection and the "network" caveat add latency. A 5-minute bound holds only in favourable cases. This is arithmetic from Apple's 500 m and 5-minute figures, not a measurement.
- **Rolling geofence.** An app could register a small region around its current position on each wake and re-arm it on exit. This raises wake frequency above significant-change, but it is still distance-based, limited to 20 conditions, needs Always authorization for background relaunch, and is an off-label use for step counting. It is a known community technique; no Apple endorsement was found.
- **Continuous location** keeps the process alive, so the app could poll pedometer data and notify within seconds of a goal. It costs a persistent blue indicator for When-In-Use apps (or Always authorization), GPS-class power, and a high App Review risk for an app that "does not otherwise need location". When the user is stationary, liveUpdates pauses, which is harmless because no steps accrue, and resumes on movement.
- **Force quit** is the weak point for every location option on iOS 26. One report says significant-change and region relaunch stopped. WWDC23 suggests liveUpdates with `CLBackgroundActivitySession` can relaunch after a user close, but this is not confirmed on iOS 26.

### Gaps
- No Apple statement was found on how long an app runs after a significant-change, visit or region relaunch. Commonly repeated figures, such as "about 10 seconds", could not be sourced.
- No Apple confirmation or denial was found of the iOS 26 change to force-quit relaunch for significant-change and region monitoring. Physical-device testing on iOS 26.x is needed.
- Measured region-monitoring latency and minimum effective radius on current iOS were not found.

## App Store Review Guidelines (2.5.4, 2.5.1, 2.4.2, 5.1.1, 5.1.5, 4.5.4)

### Takeaway
The guidelines limit background modes to "their intended purposes". Location may be used "only when it is directly relevant to the features and services provided by the app", and apps must request only data "relevant to the core functionality". A step app declaring `location` (or `audio`) only to stay alive or to wake for step counting carries a high rejection risk. App Review's documented 2.5.4 rejections ask reviewers to find "features… that require persistent location". Apple DTS explicitly warns against repurposing background mechanisms.

### Cited Findings
- 2.5.4: "Multitasking apps may only use background services for their intended purposes: VoIP, audio playback, location, task completion, local notifications, etc." — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 2.5.1 (excerpt): "Apps should use APIs and frameworks for their intended purposes and indicate that integration in their app description… HealthKit should be used for health and fitness purposes and integrate with the Health app." — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 2.4.2: "Design your app to use power efficiently… Apps should not rapidly drain battery, generate excessive heat, or put unnecessary strain on device resources… may not run unrelated background processes, such as cryptocurrency mining." — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.1.1(iii) Data Minimization: "Apps should only request access to data relevant to the core functionality of the app and should only collect and use data that is required to accomplish the relevant task." — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.1.1(iv) Access: "Apps must respect the user's permission settings and not attempt to manipulate, trick, or force people to consent to unnecessary data access… if a user declines to share Location, offer the ability to manually enter an address." — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.1.5 Location Services: "Use Location Services in your app only when it is directly relevant to the features and services provided by the app… Ensure that you notify and obtain consent before collecting, transmitting, or using location data. If your app uses Location Services, be sure to explain the purpose in your app." — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 4.5.4 Push Notifications: "Push Notifications must not be required for the app to function, and should not be used to send sensitive personal or confidential information… Abuse of these services may result in revocation of your privileges." — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- In the fetched guideline text, VoIP appears only in 2.5.4. The text beyond about 100,000 characters was not read, so a PushKit-specific clause cannot be ruled out. PushKit's own documentation is restrictive (see the "Other passive triggers" section). — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Documented rejection text (Dec 2024): "Guideline 2.5.4 - Performance - Software Requirements. The app continues to declare support for location in the UIBackgroundModes key in your Info.plist file but we are unable to locate any features besides employee tracking that require persistent location… it would be appropriate to revise the app to include additional features for your users that require the persistent use of real-time location updates while the app is in the background". An App Review staff member replied in the thread. — [Forums thread 771202](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/771202)
- Apple DTS: "Each of these mechanisms fulfils a specific purpose. Do not attempt to use them for some other purpose. Before using a background API, read clause 2.5.4 of the App Review Guidelines." — [Forums thread 685525](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/685525)
- Apple lists "Playing silent audio" and "Performing location updates" among "common causes of wasted energy in background apps". — [Apple Energy Efficiency Guide: Work Less in the Background (archived)](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/Performance/Conceptual/EnergyGuide-iOS/WorkLessInTheBackground.html)
- Apple's sanctioned use of the audio mode: "If you're building a music player, use the `audio` background mode to continue playing after the user has moved your app to the background." — [Forums thread 685525](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/685525)
- Anecdotal, from a search summary only (the thread was not fetched): an app with a user-initiated text-to-speech feature was rejected 3 times under 2.5.4 because the reviewer said audio "does not play in the background as intended". — [Forums thread 826849](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/826849)

### Inferences
- The guidelines say nothing explicit about step counting or about location used as a wake-up timer. The applicable tests are "intended purposes" (2.5.4), "directly relevant to the features" (5.1.5) and "data relevant to the core functionality" (5.1.1(iii)). An app that asks for Always location or declares the `location` background mode only to wake for step goals fails all three on their face, unless it ships a real location feature such as route tracking or walk maps, which would also change Honoured's privacy disclosures.
- Significant-change or region monitoring without the `location` background mode avoids the "persistent location" wording in the rejection template. It still needs Always authorization, which reviewers and users scrutinise under 5.1.1 and 5.1.5. Rejection risk is lower than with continuous location but still material.
- Silent-audio keep-alive is the riskiest pattern: Apple names it as energy waste and App Review rejects audio modes without audible content.

### Gaps
- No documented App Review rejection specific to a step-counting app using location or audio for wake-ups was found. The evidence is general 2.5.4 rejections plus guideline text.
- The guideline text beyond about 100,000 characters was not read.

## Battery impact

### Takeaway
Apple ranks location services from most to least power-efficient: Visits, then significant-change (cell and Wi-Fi, no GPS), then standard or continuous updates (most power). It also warns that significant-change runs "around the clock" and can cost more energy if misused. Silent pushes, app refresh, Live Activity pushes and widget reloads all run under system energy budgets for battery reasons. Apart from one vendor claim, no independent per-hour measurements were found.

### Cited Findings
- Visits are "the most power-efficient way to get location data". Significant-change "offers a low-power way… uses the cellular and Wi-Fi radios (not GPS)". Standard location "provides the most precise and regular location data, but uses more power than the other services." — [Apple: Getting the current location of a device](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/getting-the-current-location-of-a-device)
- Significant-change "can actually result in higher energy use if not employed effectively". Region and visit monitoring "are sufficient for most use cases". — [Apple Energy Efficiency Guide: Location Best Practices (archived)](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/Performance/Conceptual/EnergyGuide-iOS/LocationBestPractices.html)
- "Use background execution modes sparingly because overuse can negatively impact device performance and battery life." — [Apple: Configuring background execution modes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/configuring-background-execution-modes)
- liveUpdates "contributes to battery life by automatically pausing and resuming the updates" when stationary. — [WWDC23 session 10180](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10180/)
- WWDC20: each app has system energy and data budgets that deplete through the day, and push rate-limiting exists because running on every push "could cause significant battery-life issues". — [WWDC20 session 10063](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10063/) ([transcript mirror](https://nonstrict.eu/wwdcindex/wwdc2020/10063/))
- WWDC25: "The most fundamental constraint is energy… background execution isn't guaranteed. Instead, it's opportunistic, often discretionary, and tightly managed." — [WWDC25 session 227](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/227/)
- High-priority Live Activity pushes are budgeted "due to their impact on the user's battery life". — [WWDC23 "Update Live Activities with push notifications"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10185/)
- Widget reload budgets exist because "Reloading widgets consumes system resources and causes battery drain". — [Apple: Keeping a widget up to date](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/keeping-a-widget-up-to-date)
- Vendor measurement claim: Radar says its presets wake "while the user is moving (usually every few minutes)" and that "For most users, background tracking with these presets uses only 1-2% battery per day (measured in absolute percentage)". The iOS Battery screen shows each app's share of the session's drain, not an absolute percentage. — [Radar FAQ](https://docs.radar.com/faqs.md)

### Inferences
- For Honoured, significant-change or region wake-ups are likely cheap in radio terms, because they reuse location work the OS already does. Continuous location with a background indicator is the expensive option, and it carries both review and user-perception cost because of the blue pill.
- Fetching a step count on each wake (CMPedometer or HealthKit) is cheap. The cost lies in the wake mechanism, not the step read.

### Gaps
- No independent, methodologically clear measurements of significant-change, region or continuous background location drain on iOS 17–26 were found.
- No Apple numbers exist for push or app-refresh energy budgets.

## Other passive triggers (Live Activities, PushKit, audio, Bluetooth, Screen Time, NSE, widgets, HealthKit)

### Takeaway
None of these gives a passive, step-driven wake within 5 minutes:
- Push-to-start Live Activities (iOS 17.2+) and ActivityKit pushes are server-driven and need the server to know the step state.
- PushKit is forbidden without CallKit.
- The audio mode is for audible playback.
- Screen Time (DeviceActivity) triggers on app-usage schedules and thresholds under a restricted entitlement.
- A Notification Service Extension runs only for visible alert pushes.
- Widget reloads are budgeted at about 15–60 minutes.
- HealthKit background delivery for `stepCount` is capped at hourly on iOS.

Also, any code woken while the phone is locked generally cannot read HealthKit, because the store is encrypted while locked.

### Cited Findings
**HealthKit background delivery, the nearest step-driven wake**
- "HealthKit wakes your app whenever a process saves or deletes samples of the specified type. The system wakes your app at most once per time period defined by the specified frequency. Some sample types have a maximum frequency of [hourly]… on iOS, [stepCount] samples have an hourly maximum frequency." It requires the `com.apple.developer.healthkit.background-delivery` entitlement on iOS 15+. If the completion handler is not called, HealthKit uses backoff, and "If your app fails to respond three times, HealthKit assumes your app can't receive data and stops sending background updates." — [Apple: enableBackgroundDelivery(for:frequency:withCompletion:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/enablebackgrounddelivery(for:frequency:withcompletion:))
- Locked device: "the device encrypts the HealthKit store when the user locks the device. As a result, your app may not be able to read data from the store when it runs in the background. However, your app can still write to the store, even when the phone is locked." — [Apple: Protecting user privacy (HealthKit)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy)
- `HKError.Code.errorDatabaseInaccessible`: "The HealthKit data is unavailable because it's protected and the device is locked. This error occurs when your app queries for HealthKit data while the device is locked." — [Apple: errorDatabaseInaccessible](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkerror/code/errordatabaseinaccessible)

**Core Motion (CMPedometer), a step source without a wake trigger**
- `startUpdates(from:withHandler:)`: "When the app is suspended, the delivery of updates stops temporarily. Upon returning to foreground or background execution, the pedometer object begins updates again." — [Apple: CMPedometer.startUpdates(from:withHandler:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmpedometer/startupdates(from:withhandler:))
- History: "Only the past seven days worth of data is stored and available for you to retrieve." — [Apple: queryPedometerData(from:to:withHandler:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmpedometer/querypedometerdata(from:to:withhandler:)). It requires `NSMotionUsageDescription`. — [Apple: CMPedometer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmpedometer)

**Live Activities and push-to-start**
- "On devices that run iOS or iPadOS 17.1 and earlier, you can't start Live Activities with ActivityKit push notifications." With push-to-start, "When the system receives the ActivityKit push notification on a device, it starts a new Live Activity, wakes up your app, and grants it background runtime to allow you to download assets that the Live Activity needs." Broadcast pushes cannot start one. — [Apple: Starting and updating Live Activities with ActivityKit push notifications](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/starting-and-updating-live-activities-with-activitykit-push-notifications)
- Budget: "The system allows for a certain budget of ActivityKit push notifications per hour." Priority 10 counts toward the budget. Priority 5 "doesn't count toward the budget". `NSSupportsLiveActivitiesFrequentUpdates` raises the budget, and users can turn it off in Settings. — [same Apple article](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/starting-and-updating-live-activities-with-activitykit-push-notifications). WWDC23: "there is no limit on how many updates you can send" at low priority, but they "might not be updated immediately". — [WWDC23 session 10185](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10185/)
- Lifetime: a Live Activity "can be active for up to eight hours", then stays on the Lock Screen up to 4 more hours, 12 hours in total. "In general, your app needs to be in the foreground to start a Live Activity. You can update or end a Live Activity from your app while it runs in the background". — [Apple: Displaying live data with Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities)
- Anecdotal, from search summaries only (threads not fetched): after a force quit, push-to-start still renders the activity, but `pushTokenUpdates` never fires, so the server cannot update or end it. A separate report says push-to-start tokens went missing after force-close for "close to half of our users". — [Forums thread 834923](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/834923); [Forums thread 805324](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/805324)

**PushKit / VoIP**
- "For apps built using the iOS 13 SDK or later, PushKit requires you to use CallKit when handling VoIP calls… If you are unable to support CallKit in your app, you cannot use PushKit to handle push notifications. Instead… use a notification service extension". — [Apple: Responding to VoIP notifications from PushKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/pushkit/responding-to-voip-notifications-from-pushkit)

**Audio background mode**
- Apple's intended use is a music player continuing playback. "Playing silent audio" is listed as an energy waste. — [Forums thread 685525](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/685525); [Energy Guide: Work Less in the Background](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/Performance/Conceptual/EnergyGuide-iOS/WorkLessInTheBackground.html)

**Notification Service Extension**
- "Notification service app extensions only operate on remote notifications configured in the system to display an alert to the user." The payload "must include the `mutable-content` key with a value of `1`" and "an `alert` dictionary". The extension has "only about 30 seconds". — [Apple: Modifying content in newly delivered notifications](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/modifying-content-in-newly-delivered-notifications)
- Apple positions the NSE as the way to "perform a task in the background and show a notification to the user". — [Apple: Choosing background strategies for your app](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/choosing-background-strategies-for-your-app)
- The filtering entitlement "allows a notification service extension to receive remote notifications without displaying the notification to the user". It must be applied for, and silencing requires `apns-push-type: alert`. — [Apple: com.apple.developer.usernotifications.filtering](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/entitlements/com.apple.developer.usernotifications.filtering)

**Widgets / WidgetKit**
- "For a widget the user frequently views, a daily budget typically includes from 40 to 70 refreshes. This rate roughly translates to widget reloads every 15 to 60 minutes". Timeline entries should be "at least about 5 minutes apart". Location-using widgets are reloaded "after a significant location change happens". — [Apple: Keeping a widget up to date](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/keeping-a-widget-up-to-date)

**Screen Time / Focus (DeviceActivity, FamilyControls)**
- DeviceActivity lets "Your app extension… receive warnings before an activity's schedule starts or ends, or when an activity is about to reach a predefined threshold" of app or website usage. — [Apple: DeviceActivity](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/deviceactivity)
- It requires the Family Controls capability, and "Before submitting your app to the App Store, you must [request] to use the entitlement." — [Apple: FamilyControls](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/familycontrols)

**Timers**
- For timer apps Apple points to AlarmKit, or to local notifications on older systems. Both are time-based, not step-based. — [Forums thread 685525](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/685525)

### Inferences
- **HealthKit background delivery** is the step-driven wake. On iOS it is capped at hourly for `stepCount`, and when the device is locked a wake may be unable to read the HealthKit sum (`errorDatabaseInaccessible`). That limits any HealthKit-based goal check while the phone is locked in a pocket, whichever mechanism wakes the app. A woken process would need `CMPedometer` (iPhone-only steps, not wearable steps) or a value cached before the lock.
- **Server-driven channels** (visible alert push with NSE, ActivityKit push, push-to-start) can reach a locked phone quickly and are not subject to silent-push throttling in the same way. They fit only if the server already knows the step total from a non-iPhone source. A visible alert push is the most reliable server-to-lock-screen path, and per the documentation it does not depend on the app being runnable.
- **Screen Time / DeviceActivity** is the wrong tool. It triggers on app usage and schedules, its entitlement is for parental and self-control use cases, and repurposing it as a timer would likely fail review (an inference from 2.5.4 and the entitlement gating).

### Gaps
- Whether `CMPedometer.queryPedometerData` works while the device is locked and the app has just been woken was not confirmed from Apple sources. A device test is required.
- Core Bluetooth state preservation and restoration (relaunch on accessory events) could not be retrieved from Apple's archived guide during this research. It does not apply to Honoured in any case, because there is no BLE accessory the app owns, and Garmin devices pair with Garmin Connect.
- No Apple statement was found on whether push-to-start wakes a force-quit app. The evidence is anecdotal.
- The minimum DeviceActivity schedule interval was not verified.

## Overall feasibility: notifying within ~5 minutes of reaching a step goal while locked

### Takeaway
No documented passive mechanism on iOS 16–26 guarantees a wake within 5 minutes of a step threshold on a locked, pocketed iPhone:
- Silent pushes allow about 2–3 per hour, are coalesced, and are blocked by a force quit and Low Power Mode.
- BGAppRefresh runs at system-predicted times with about 30 seconds of runtime.
- HealthKit `stepCount` delivery is hourly at most.
- Significant-change gives at best one event per 500 m and at least 5 minutes apart, only while covering distance, and at high review risk for a non-location app.

Only continuous background location (a persistent process) reliably achieves under 5 minutes, at the cost of Always or indicator-based location, battery, and a high 2.5.4/5.1.5 rejection risk. A HealthKit read while locked can fail regardless of the wake mechanism.

### Inferences
- **Best documented passive combination without location:** HealthKit background delivery (hourly at most) plus BGAppRefresh (opportunistic) plus silent pushes (2–3 per hour, best effort). This gives a typical latency of tens of minutes to hours, not 5 minutes, and nothing at all after a force quit or in Low Power Mode.
- **Location options:** significant-change or region can approach a 5-minute cadence only while the user covers distance outdoors. Continuous location meets the target but contradicts "does not otherwise need location" and is the configuration App Review's 2.5.4 template targets.
- **Server-side path:** if the goal crossing can be detected server-side from non-iPhone data (for example, wearable cloud sync), a visible alert push (optionally with an NSE) is the documented, unthrottled-by-silent-push-rules path to a locked lock screen. Whether such data is available within 5 minutes is outside this note's scope.
- **Validation:** a simulator cannot validate any of this. Physical-device tests on iOS 26.x should cover the force-quit, Low Power Mode, locked-phone HealthKit read, and CMPedometer-while-locked cases.

### Gaps
- No primary-source measurements of end-to-end latency, from goal crossing to notification on a locked iPhone, for any of the mechanisms were found.
- Apple has not documented the iOS 26 force-quit relaunch behaviour for location services.

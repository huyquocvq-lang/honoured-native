# HealthKit and Core Motion background limits on iPhone (iOS 16–26) for a locked-phone step-goal alert

Scope: iPhone only, iOS 16 through iOS 26, researched 2026-10-19. Apple documentation pages were read through Apple's JSON documentation endpoint (`developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/...`). The URLs below are the human-readable pages. Documentation pages carry no version history, so "current" means as published on 2026-10-19. Evidence labels: **[Apple doc]** is official documentation. **[Apple staff]** is a forum reply carrying an Apple Staff badge (DTS Engineer or Frameworks Engineer). **[Developer report]** is anecdotal. **[Snippet only]** means I could not open the page and the claim comes from a search-result excerpt.

## 1. Maximum background delivery frequency for stepCount (and distanceWalkingRunning); is there an hourly cap?

### Takeaway
Yes. Apple documents an hourly cap for step count on iOS: "on iOS, stepCount samples have an hourly maximum frequency," and the system "enforces this frequency transparently". Asking for `.immediate` does not raise an error, but the app is woken at most once an hour for step changes. Apple does not name `distanceWalkingRunning`. It says only that "some sample types" are capped at hourly, so whether distance is capped is undocumented.

### Cited Findings
- **[Apple doc]** The `frequency` parameter of `enableBackgroundDelivery(for:frequency:withCompletion:)` is "The maximum frequency of the updates. The system wakes your app from the background at most once per time period specified." — [enableBackgroundDelivery(for:frequency:withCompletion:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/enablebackgrounddelivery(for:frequency:withcompletion:))
- **[Apple doc]** Verbatim from the Discussion: "HealthKit wakes your app whenever a process saves or deletes samples of the specified type. The system wakes your app at most once per time period defined by the specified frequency. Some sample types have a maximum frequency of hourly. The system enforces this frequency transparently. For example, on iOS, stepCount samples have an hourly maximum frequency." — [enableBackgroundDelivery](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/enablebackgrounddelivery(for:frequency:withcompletion:))
- **[Apple doc]** watchOS differences, verbatim: "In watchOS, most data types have an hourly maximum frequency; however, the following data types can receive updates at immediate: highHeartRateEvent, lowHeartRateEvent, irregularHeartRhythmEvent, environmentalAudioExposureEvent, headphoneAudioExposureEvent, lowCardioFitnessEvent, numberOfTimesFallen, vo2Max, handwashingEvent, toothbrushingEvent." Also: "in watchOS, the background updates share a budget with WKApplicationRefreshBackgroundTask tasks. Your app can receive four updates (or background app refresh tasks) an hour, as long as it has a complication on the active watch face." stepCount is not on the watchOS immediate list. — [enableBackgroundDelivery](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/enablebackgrounddelivery(for:frequency:withcompletion:))
- **[Apple doc]** Entitlement: "For iOS 15 and watchOS 8 and later, you must enable the HealthKit Background Delivery by adding the com.apple.developer.healthkit.background-delivery entitlement to your app. If your app doesn't have this entitlement, the enableBackgroundDelivery(for:frequency:withCompletion:) method fails with an errorAuthorizationDenied error." — [enableBackgroundDelivery](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/enablebackgrounddelivery(for:frequency:withcompletion:))
- **[Apple doc]** `HKUpdateFrequency` is defined as "Constants that determine how often the system launches your app in response to changes to HealthKit data." Its cases are `immediate` ("The system launches your app every time it detects a change."), `hourly` ("…at most once an hour in response to changes."), `daily` ("…at most once a day…"), and `weekly` ("…at most once per week…"). The enum page does not mention the per-type caps, which appear only on the `enableBackgroundDelivery` page. — [HKUpdateFrequency](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkupdatefrequency)
- **[Apple doc]** Apple's wording is "at most", a ceiling and not a guarantee: "HealthKit notifies your app at most once per time period defined by the frequency you specified when registering." — [Executing Observer Queries](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/executing-observer-queries)
- **[Apple doc]** Completion-handler backoff: if the app doesn't call the completion handler, "HealthKit continues to attempt to launch your app using a backoff algorithm to increase the delay between attempts. If your app fails to respond three times, HealthKit assumes your app can't receive data and stops sending background updates." — [enableBackgroundDelivery](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/enablebackgrounddelivery(for:frequency:withcompletion:))
- **[Apple doc]** "Background server queries aren't supported on the Simulator. Be sure to test your background queries on a device." — [HKObserverQuery](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkobserverquery)
- **[Developer report]** The system scheduler `dasd` further gates background-delivery launches. In iOS 15, developers posted logs of `com.apple.healthkit.background-delivery.<bundle>` jobs refused with `ApplicationPolicy … Decision: Must Not Proceed`. One developer then reported launches "less frequently on iOS 15 … Sometimes days without a background launch". Another saw launches "every few days using .hourly data types". An Apple Frameworks Engineer acknowledged a fix in iOS 15.2 (Dec 2021). Later posters reported similar failures on iOS 16.0, 17.3 and 18.0, often noting that delivery works while attached to Xcode but not otherwise. — [HKObserverQuery BackgroundDelivery not executed (thread 690974)](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/690974)

### Inferences
- With step count capped at hourly on iOS, HealthKit background delivery alone cannot meet a "within ~5 minutes" goal, even with the phone unlocked. In the worst case the next wake is about 60 minutes after the previous one, and the scheduler can delay it further. Honoured's `.immediate` registration for `stepCount` is silently treated as hourly.
- The cap is per sample type. A change to a type with no documented cap could wake the app sooner. Apple does not list which iOS types are capped, though, and this only helps if another type is written at the moment the goal is crossed.

### Gaps
- Apple doesn't say whether `distanceWalkingRunning` (or flights climbed, active energy) has an hourly cap on iOS. The doc names only `stepCount` as the iOS example.
- Apple doesn't say whether the hourly window is a rolling 60 minutes from the last wake or is aligned to clock hours.
- I found no iOS 26-specific change to these limits. The doc page has no version history.

## 2. When and how often does the iPhone write its own pedometer step samples into HealthKit?

### Takeaway
I found no Apple documentation or Apple-engineer statement on how often the iPhone's motion coprocessor data is written into HealthKit. Only anecdotal reports exist. They suggest the delay grew from near real time (iOS 14) to minutes (iOS 15 and later), and one user reported 17 minutes. That fits Honoured's own measurement of 3–8 minutes. Apps that read `CMPedometer` directly see fresher counts than HealthKit.

### Cited Findings
- **[Developer report / user forum]** An iPhone-only user (iPhone 6S and 12 mini, no Apple Watch) reported that the step count "used to update several times a minute" on iOS 14, but on iOS 15 "It can take minutes for the count to change" (Sep 2021). — [Apple Community thread 253198975](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/253198975)
- **[Developer report / user forum]** In the same thread (Feb 2022), on an iPhone 13 Pro with iOS 15.3: "My step counter took 17 minutes to update on the Health app." No Apple staff replied. One user only speculated that Apple might "reduce the frequency of reading the pedo chip, as a way to save power". — [Apple Community thread 253198975](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/253198975)
- **[Developer report]** In the same thread, the original poster said a third-party pedometer app (reading Core Motion directly) "updates within seconds". Turning on that app's Apple Health sync brought back the slow counting. — [Apple Community thread 253198975](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/253198975)
- **[Snippet only, third-party FAQ]** A step-rewards app's FAQ reportedly says Apple Health pushes data about every 2–3 minutes to save battery. The page returned HTTP 403 and could not be checked. — [WeWard FAQ](https://faq.weward.fr/hc/en-us/articles/29040069407122-Tip-for-forcing-step-synchronization-on-iPhone)
- **[Snippet only, developer forum]** A 2018 thread reported that per-minute iPhone step samples looked "evenly averaged" after iOS 11, where earlier versions showed real per-minute counts. This suggests iPhone samples are written as batched intervals, not live. — [Compressed step count data from iOS 11 HealthKit (thread 98966)](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/98966)

### Inferences
- The HealthKit write delay (Honoured measured 3–8 minutes; one user reported up to 17) uses most or all of a 5-minute budget before any HealthKit-based mechanism could even fire. Combined with the hourly cap from question 1, HealthKit is a poor trigger for a 5-minute alert.
- `CMPedometer` is fresher than HealthKit while the app is running, because it reads the coprocessor directly. That only helps if the app is executing (see question 4).

### Gaps
- No official batching interval, and no published controlled measurement (stating device, iOS version and method) for iOS 16–26.
- No source says whether iPhone step samples are written to HealthKit at a different cadence while the device is locked. Writes while locked go to a temporary cache (question 3), but the write cadence itself is undocumented.

## 3. Are HealthKit background deliveries made while the device is locked? Data protection and errorDatabaseInaccessible

### Takeaway
No. Apple documents that the HealthKit store is encrypted when the device locks and that apps cannot read it. An Apple Frameworks Engineer stated that, while the device is locked, observer queries get no callbacks and background delivery does not launch the app until the device unlocks. That matches Honoured's observation that callbacks were queued and delivered at unlock. Writes are still allowed and are cached until unlock. The one exception Apple documents is data from an active iPhone workout session (iOS 26), after a one-time user prompt.

### Cited Findings
- **[Apple doc]** "The user's device stores all HealthKit data locally. For security, the device encrypts the HealthKit store when the user locks the device. As a result, your app may not be able to read data from the store when it runs in the background. However, your app can still write to the store, even when the phone is locked. HealthKit temporarily caches the data and saves it to the encrypted store as soon as the user unlocks the phone." — [Protecting user privacy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy)
- **[Apple doc]** `HKError.Code.errorDatabaseInaccessible`: "The HealthKit data is unavailable because it's protected and the device is locked." Discussion: "This error occurs when your app queries for HealthKit data while the device is locked. You can, however, still save data. This data is saved into a temporary file, which is merged with HealthKit's data when the user unlocks their device." — [errorDatabaseInaccessible](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkerror/code/errordatabaseinaccessible)
- **[Apple staff — Frameworks Engineer, Nov 2021]** "HealthKit data is not available while the device is locked. You can start your HKObserverQuery while locked, but you will not receive callbacks and, if you are using background delivery, your app will not be launched for new data until the device unlocks and the Health database is available." — [Can we start an HKObserverQuery and read data when notified when the phone is locked? (thread 694223)](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/694223)
- **[Apple staff — DTS Engineer Ziqiao Chen, May 2026, accepted answer]** "For privacy reasons, your app is not allowed to read health data while a device is locked, though it can still save data, which is then saved into a temporary file and merged with HealthKit's data when the user unlocks their device." He added that for an iOS workout session, "the first time a workout session is started, the system will show a prompt indicating that workout data will be available to your app, even while the device is locked." The question asked specifically about near real-time sync while locked. — [Clarification on HealthKit Background Access While Device Is Locked (thread 824819)](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/824819)
- **[Developer report, system log]** A developer's iOS 15.1 beta log shows `dasd` refusing a HealthKit background-delivery job with `FileProtectionPolicy … Decision: Absolutely Must Not Proceed … Rationale: [{classALocked == 1 AND activityFileProtection == <File Protection: ClassA>}]`. In other words, the job is tagged with Class A (complete) file protection and cannot run while Class A data is locked. — [thread 690974](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/690974)
- **[Apple doc / WWDC25 session 322]** "For privacy reasons, health data is not normally available while a device is locked, but never fear. The first time a workout session is started, the system will show a prompt indicating that workout data will be available to your app, even while the device is locked." Also: "unlike Apple Watch, your iPhone will most likely lock while a workout is running", and Apple suggests a Live Activity "to show live updates right from the lock screen without the user having to unlock their phone." "Once the phone is unlocked, HealthKit will save the workout." Steps are not named among the generated types; distance and calories are. — [WWDC25: Track workouts with HealthKit on iOS and iPadOS](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/322/)
- **[Developer report]** "The HealthKit datastore is inaccessible when the device is locked, so you will not get any updates at that time" (community reply, no Apple badge, May 2023). The question was about workouts with `.immediate` delivery and a Face ID lock. — [thread 730202](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/730202)
- **[Developer report]** "iOS background HKObserverQueries with HKSampleQueries … come unstuck with no access to health data on lock" (Jan 2016). — [thread 30084](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/30084)
- **[Apple staff — Quinn "The Eskimo!", DTS, revised 2026-01-09]** On `BGAppRefreshTask`: "Folks assume that app refresh will provide regular background execution time. That's not the case. The system applies a range of heuristics to decide which apps get app refresh time and when." And: "if you expect that the app refresh mechanism will grant you background execution time, say, every 15 minutes, you'll be disappointed. In fact, there are common scenarios where it won't grant you any background execution time at all!" The post's list of background mechanisms (silent push, BGAppRefreshTaskRequest, BGProcessingTaskRequest, BGContinuedProcessingTask, UIApplication background task, URLSession background session, plus special-purpose modes such as audio) does not mention HealthKit or Core Motion. — [iOS Background Execution Limits (thread 685525)](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/685525)

### Inferences
- While the phone is locked, HealthKit gives no runtime and no readable data. Even if another mechanism, such as a silent push or `BGAppRefreshTask`, woke the app while locked, a HealthKit query would fail with `errorDatabaseInaccessible`. A HealthKit-based "goal reached" check therefore cannot run until unlock. This matches Honoured's on-device observation.
- The iOS 26 iPhone `HKWorkoutSession` is the only documented way to get HealthKit workout data while locked. It requires the user to start a workout and accept the prompt, so it doesn't suit passive all-day step tracking. Apple's session doesn't say whether step count is delivered live.
- Writes by the iPhone itself while locked go to a temporary cache and merge at unlock. So even the "3–8 minutes to reach HealthKit" figure may not hold while locked. The samples may become visible to any reader only at unlock. (Not directly sourced; see Gaps.)

### Gaps
- Apple doesn't say whether the system's own iPhone step writes during lock land in the temporary cache or in a store visible to other processes before unlock.
- No Apple statement gives how soon after locking the store becomes inaccessible. (Class A keys are generally discarded shortly after lock; I found no HealthKit-specific figure.)
- WWDC25 session 322 doesn't explicitly state the iPhone background-execution rules during a workout session, or whether `stepCount` is among the live-collected types on iPhone.

## 4. Core Motion: does CMPedometer work in the background or on a background launch while locked? Is pedometer data protected (first unlock vs every unlock)?

### Takeaway
Per Apple's documentation, live `CMPedometer` updates stop while the app is suspended and resume whenever the app is executing again, in the foreground or background. The motion coprocessor keeps counting regardless and stores 7 days of history, which `queryPedometerData` can read later. That matches Honoured's observation that the count was readable at unlock. Apple doesn't document a data-protection class for pedometer data, or whether `queryPedometerData` works while locked. I found no Apple-engineer statement on it.

### Cited Findings
- **[Apple doc]** `startUpdates(from:withHandler:)`: "When the app is suspended, the delivery of updates stops temporarily. Upon returning to foreground or background execution, the pedometer object begins updates again." The handler "is called repeatedly on a background thread as new data arrives". Apple gives no interval. — [CMPedometer.startUpdates(from:withHandler:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmpedometer/startupdates(from:withhandler:))
- **[Apple doc]** `queryPedometerData(from:to:withHandler:)`: "Use this method to retrieve historical pedestrian data between the specified dates… Only the past seven days worth of data is stored and available for you to retrieve." The page says nothing about locked or background behavior. — [CMPedometer.queryPedometerData](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmpedometer/querypedometerdata(from:to:withhandler:))
- **[Apple doc]** CMPedometer overview: "The pedometer object manages a cache of historic data that you can query or you can ask for live updates as the data is processed." `NSMotionUsageDescription` is required: "If you don't include a usage description string, your app crashes when you call this API." The overview says nothing about background execution, locked devices or data protection. — [CMPedometer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmpedometer)
- **[Apple doc]** `CMMotionActivityManager.startActivityUpdates`: "The handler block is executed on a best effort basis and updates are not delivered while your app is suspended. If updates arrived while your app was suspended, the last update is delivered to your app when it resumes execution." — [startActivityUpdates(to:withHandler:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmmotionactivitymanager/startactivityupdates(to:withhandler:))
- **[Apple staff — Quinn, DTS]** By default iOS suspends an app "shortly after the user has moved it to the background; this suspension prevents the process from running any code." — [iOS Background Execution Limits (thread 685525)](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/685525)
- **[Snippet only, developer report]** One developer reported that Pedometer API promises "never resolve" when called from a location background task. This was an Expo / React Native wrapper, not native Swift. — [Expo forums](https://forums.expo.dev/t/pedometer-api-promises-never-resolve-in-location-background-tasks/22843)
- **[Snippet only, developer report]** A developer's pull request reports that cadence capture from `CMPedometer` stopped when the screen locked, and was fixed by adding background location so the app kept running. — [STRDR9/CadenceOptimizer PR #3](https://github.com/STRDR9/CadenceOptimizer/pull/3)

### Inferences
- `CMPedometer` delivers data only to code that is running. On a locked iPhone a normal app is suspended, so nothing evaluates the step count until some other mechanism gives the app execution time. Apple's wording "foreground or background execution" means that if the app is legitimately running in the background (for example, an active workout session or a background mode), `startUpdates` should keep firing. Whether that holds while locked depends on Core Motion data protection, which is undocumented. The two snippet-only reports are mixed: one hang, and one capture that worked once location kept the app alive.
- Honoured's observation (count kept accumulating and was readable at unlock) fits the documented 7-day coprocessor cache. It doesn't show whether a query made while locked would succeed.

### Gaps
- I found no Apple documentation, WWDC statement or DTS answer on the data-protection class of Core Motion pedometer or activity history (until-first-unlock vs complete), or on `queryPedometerData` behavior while locked. This needs an on-device test: run a query from a background-running context after lock and log the result and error.
- No documented live-update interval for `startUpdates`.

## 5. Does Core Motion offer any way to wake or launch an app when a step-count threshold is crossed?

### Takeaway
No such mechanism is documented. CMPedometer's API surface is availability and authorization checks, live updates, pause/resume event updates, and historical queries. None of them launches or resumes a suspended app. Pedometer events report only "pause" and "resume", not thresholds, and Apple's DTS list of background-launch mechanisms doesn't include Core Motion. This conclusion rests on negative evidence. I found no explicit Apple sentence saying "Core Motion cannot launch your app".

### Cited Findings
- **[Apple doc]** CMPedometer methods: `isStepCountingAvailable()`, `isDistanceAvailable()`, `isFloorCountingAvailable()`, `isPaceAvailable()`, `isCadenceAvailable()`, `isPedometerEventTrackingAvailable()`, `authorizationStatus()`, `startUpdates(from:withHandler:)`, `stopUpdates()`, `startEventUpdates(handler:)`, `stopEventUpdates()`, and `queryPedometerData(from:to:withHandler:)`. None takes a threshold or goal parameter. — [CMPedometer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmpedometer)
- **[Apple doc]** `startEventUpdates(handler:)`: "Pedometer events report changes in the user's pedestrian activity." Available from iOS 10. — [startEventUpdates(handler:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmpedometer/starteventupdates(handler:))
- **[Apple doc]** `CMPedometerEventType` has only two cases: `pause` ("The user's pedestrian activity stopped.") and `resume` ("The user's pedestrian activity resumed."). — [CMPedometerEventType](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmpedometereventtype)
- **[Apple doc]** Live updates stop while the app is suspended. Pedometer updates resume only "upon returning to foreground or background execution", and activity updates are "not delivered while your app is suspended". — [startUpdates](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmpedometer/startupdates(from:withhandler:)); [startActivityUpdates](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmmotionactivitymanager/startactivityupdates(to:withhandler:))
- **[Apple staff — Quinn, DTS]** The general-purpose ways to run or resume an app in the background are silent push, `BGAppRefreshTaskRequest`, `BGProcessingTaskRequest`, `BGContinuedProcessingTask`, a `UIApplication` background task and `URLSession` background sessions, plus special-purpose modes. Core Motion is not mentioned. — [iOS Background Execution Limits (thread 685525)](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/685525)

### Inferences
- Core Motion cannot itself trigger a notification at a step threshold on a locked iPhone. Any design using CMPedometer for "goal reached" needs separate runtime, from a background mode, a workout session, or another wake source, and must then compare `queryPedometerData` or `startUpdates` totals against the goal. The notification latency would be set by that wake source, not by Core Motion.
- The "resume"/"pause" events are tied to walking state. Even if they were delivered to a running app, they don't correspond to a step count.

### Gaps
- No explicit Apple statement (doc or DTS) that "Core Motion does not launch apps". The conclusion is inferred from the documented API surface and the suspension notes.
- The CMPedometerEvent docs don't say whether events that occur during suspension are delivered (or coalesced) when the app resumes.

## 6. Does HealthKit or Core Motion background delivery relaunch an app the user force-quit?

### Takeaway
HealthKit: no. Apple DTS says that not relaunching a force-quit app is as designed, that HealthKit background delivery is not one of the exceptions, and that the block lasts until the user next opens the app manually. Core Motion has no background-launch mechanism to begin with (question 5), so force-quit is moot there. The pedometer keeps recording into its 7-day history regardless, and the app can read it on next launch.

### Cited Findings
- **[Apple staff — DTS Engineer Ziqiao Chen, Feb 2025, accepted answer]** "I believe this is an as-designed behavior. In most cases, the system does not relaunch an app after it's force quit … because force quitting an app is interpreted as a strong sign that the user doesn't want it running. This rule has some exceptions, but HealthKit background deliver isn't one of them." He also said there is no current documentation of this; it was once in the "iPhoneOS Programming Guide" and was lost in a documentation reorganization. — [Healthkit background delivery when app is not running (thread 775325)](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/775325)
- **[Apple staff — Engineer, Oct 2025]** In reply to a step-count `HKObserverQuery` with `.immediate` that stopped after a force-quit, the engineer quoted Quinn: "iOS also sets a flag that prevents the app from being launched in the background. That flag gets cleared when the user next launches the app manually… there's no documented way for your app to override the user's choice." He suggested a push notification to prompt the user to reopen the app, or a Notification Service Extension that tries to read HealthKit, with the caveat that it runs "in a limited memory, time, and access sandbox". — [Unable to receive HealthKit updates when app is force-quit (thread 803365)](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/803365)
- **[Apple staff — Quinn, DTS]** "In some circumstances iOS will not honour this flag. The exact cases where this happens are not documented and have changed over time." — [iOS Background Execution Limits (thread 685525)](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/685525)
- **[Developer report]** A developer on iOS 15.2 saw the `dasd` "Must Not Proceed" HealthKit background-delivery messages and believed they "only occur if the app has been force quit by the user" (Nov 2021). — [thread 690974](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/690974)
- **[Apple doc]** Core Motion: `CMPedometer` "manages a cache of historic data", and up to 7 days can be queried later, so steps taken while the app was force-quit are still retrievable on next launch. — [CMPedometer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmpedometer); [queryPedometerData](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmpedometer/querypedometerdata(from:to:withhandler:))

### Inferences
- For Honoured, a force-quit disables every background path (HealthKit delivery, `BGAppRefreshTask`) until the user reopens the app. A Notification Service Extension woken by a push would still face the HealthKit lock restriction from question 3 if the phone is locked.
- The Oct 2025 suggestion of server push plus a Notification Service Extension that reads HealthKit is not a locked-phone solution, because HealthKit reads fail while locked.

### Gaps
- No current Apple documentation page states the force-quit rule. The only sources are DTS forum answers, and Apple staff confirmed the documentation is missing.
- No source says whether a device reboot clears the force-quit flag for HealthKit background delivery.

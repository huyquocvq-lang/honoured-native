# User-initiated and semi-passive iPhone mechanisms for live step counting and a fast step-goal notification while the phone is locked

Research date: 2026-10-19. Today iOS 27 is the current release (WWDC26 APIs such as `LongRunningIntent` are documented as 27.0). The brief covers iOS 16–26; iOS 27-only items are labelled. "Documented" means Apple documentation, a WWDC transcript, Apple sample code, or a reply from an Apple engineer. "Anecdotal" means a developer report.

A fact used by several sections below: Apple's `CMPedometer.startUpdates(from:withHandler:)` documentation says "When the app is suspended, the delivery of updates stops temporarily. Upon returning to foreground or background execution, the pedometer object begins updates again." — [Apple: CMPedometer.startUpdates](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmpedometer/startupdates(from:withhandler:)). Live step counting while locked therefore needs some mechanism that keeps the app process running (not suspended). The same page gives no update interval; it only says the handler is called "regularly" "as new data arrives". The page is available from iOS 8 — same URL.

## 1. HKWorkoutSession on iPhone/iPad (iOS 26): background runtime, background mode, live steps, location, indicator, duration, App Review

### Takeaway
From iOS 26 an iPhone app can start its own `HKWorkoutSession` with an `HKLiveWorkoutBuilder`. Apple designs it for workouts that keep running while the iPhone is locked: it shows a one-time system prompt about workout data being available while locked, relaunches the app after a crash, and its sample app keeps updating a Live Activity while locked. That makes it the strongest documented iPhone-only way to keep running during a user-started walk. Three things are less settled. Apple's background-modes table lists `workout-processing` as watchOS-only, and Apple's own iPhone sample does not declare it. Live step count from the builder is not documented for iPhone. App Review accepts workout sessions only for genuine fitness use.

### Cited Findings
**Availability**
- `HKWorkoutSession.init(healthStore:configuration:)` and `HKLiveWorkoutDataSource` are listed as iOS/iPadOS 26.0+ (watchOS 5.0+) — [Apple: HKWorkoutSession init](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkworkoutsession/init(healthstore:configuration:)); [Apple: HKLiveWorkoutDataSource](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkliveworkoutdatasource).
- The `HKWorkoutSession` class itself is listed as iOS 17.0+ because iOS 17 added *mirrored* sessions, which a Watch starts and hands to the iPhone. An iPhone could not start its own session before iOS 26 — [Apple: HKWorkoutSession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkworkoutsession); [WWDC23 "Build a multi-device workout app"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10023/). Third-party GitHub threads describe the same split: before iOS 26 an iPhone only receives sessions mirrored from a Watch — [kill-strava PR #145](https://github.com/MagicWilliams/kill-strava/pull/145) (anecdotal).

**Same API as watchOS, iPhone runs while locked**
- WWDC25 session 322: "Just like on Apple Watch, you can now use a workout session to track any activity and use the associated workout builder to save the workout in HealthKit." "Unlike Apple Watch, your iPhone will most likely lock while a workout is running." "The first time a workout session is started, the system will show a prompt indicating that workout data will be available to your app, even while the device is locked." — [WWDC25 322 "Track workouts with HealthKit on iOS and iPadOS"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/322/).
- Apple's documentation: "iPhone typically locks during workouts. For privacy reasons, health data usually isn't accessible while the device is locked. However, the system can prompt someone to provide your app access to workout data even when their device is locked. You can then display Live Activities on the Lock Screen…" — [Apple: HKWorkoutSession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkworkoutsession).
- Crash recovery: "The system will automatically relaunch your app in case of a crash. The workout session in Builder will be restored in their previous state… For iPhone and iPad, we've added a new scene delegate you'll use to recover your in-session workout." The app calls `recoverActiveWorkoutSession` and re-creates the `HKLiveWorkoutDataSource` — [WWDC25 322](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/322/).
- Siri can start, pause, resume, and cancel a workout from the Lock Screen: "You will now be able to start, pause, resume, and cancel a workout without ever having to unlock your phone… This must be handled from inside your app to work on the lock screen." — [WWDC25 322](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/322/).

**Apple's iPhone sample: background mode and entitlements** (downloaded and inspected from [Apple sample "Building a workout app for iPhone and iPad"](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/building-a-workout-app-for-iphone-and-ipad), iOS 26.0+, Xcode 26)
- The app's Info.plist declares `UIBackgroundModes` = [`processing`] only. It does **not** declare `workout-processing` or `location`. It also declares `NSSupportsLiveActivities` = YES and `INIntentsSupported` = `INStartWorkoutIntent`, `INPauseWorkoutIntent`, `INResumeWorkoutIntent`, and `INEndWorkoutIntent`.
- Entitlements: `com.apple.developer.healthkit` and `com.apple.developer.siri`. The app target's deployment target is 26.0.
- `WorkoutWidgetViewModel` creates the Live Activity with `Activity.request(... pushType: .token)`. During the workout it updates the activity locally from the app with `staleDate: Date.now + 15`, and uses `relevanceScore` 100 for alerts. The app therefore runs and updates the Live Activity while the phone is locked.
- The sample supports walking, running, cycling, rowing, and yoga, each with indoor and outdoor configurations. It reads distance (`distanceWalkingRunning` for walking/running), active and basal energy, and heart rate. It does **not** read or display `stepCount`. It uses no CoreLocation code.
- Apple's "Configuring background execution modes" table lists "Workout processing (`workout-processing`) — watchOS only": "The app uses a workout session to track a user's activity on Apple Watch." — [Apple: Configuring background execution modes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/configuring-background-execution-modes). The watchOS article says "Workout sessions require the Workout processing background mode" — [Apple: Running workout sessions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/running-workout-sessions).
- A third-party iPhone PR nonetheless adds `workout-processing` "so a workout keeps recording when the phone is locked". It was validated only in the simulator, and device testing was left as an open item — [TheRealestNwah/fitness-app PR #278](https://github.com/TheRealestNwah/fitness-app/pull/278) (anecdotal).

**Data on iPhone (steps, distance, heart rate)**
- "While all workout activity types are available on iPhone and iPad, these devices don't contain a heart rate sensor." External BLE heart-rate monitors are supported. The data source's types to collect "will contain all possible sample types… heart rate will be included even though it might not be generated by the system unless you have an external heart rate sensor." Extra types can be added with enable/disable collection — [WWDC25 322](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/322/).
- "iPhone and iPad can collect various workout metrics, but the system may generate different samples than those specifically requested by an app." — [Apple: HKWorkoutSession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkworkoutsession).
- `enableCollection(for:predicate:)` "Begins automatically calculating statistics for samples that match the quantity type and predicate." The page does not list which types each activity collects automatically — [Apple: HKLiveWorkoutDataSource](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkliveworkoutdatasource).
- On watchOS, an outdoor run "collects and saves activeEnergyBurned, basalEnergyBurned, heartRate, and distanceWalkingRunning samples". Steps are not listed — [Apple: Running workout sessions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/running-workout-sessions).
- A developer forum report (iOS 26.4–27) describes an iPhone workout builder writing "distance, active energy, and step samples" to Health. The same thread reports `finishWorkout` returning nil workout and nil error, and says that when the device is locked the workout is lost. It affects "a small percentage of users on iOS 26.4 through 26.6 and iOS 27". Apple DTS could not reproduce it (FB22396180) — [Apple Forums 825838](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/825838) (anecdotal, unresolved).

**Location / GPS**
- WWDC25 322 and the sample set only `configuration.locationType = .outdoor/.indoor`. Neither says that location permission or GPS is required, and the sample has no location background mode or usage string — [WWDC25 322](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/322/); [Apple sample](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/building-a-workout-app-for-iphone-and-ipad).

**System indicator and UI**
- Apple describes the app's own Live Activity as the Lock Screen surface ("show a live activity with your most crucial metrics on the lock screen") — [WWDC25 322](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/322/). No source describes a separate status-bar indicator for workouts.

**Duration**
- Apple documents no session duration limit. A secondary blog says "there is no hard maximum workout duration", without citation — [blakecrosley.com](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/watchos-workout-lifecycle) (secondary, uncited). The Live Activity that accompanies the session has an 8-hour active limit — see §3.

**App Review**
- Guideline 2.5.4: "Multitasking apps may only use background services for their intended purposes: VoIP, audio playback, location, task completion, local notifications, etc." Guideline 2.5.1: "HealthKit should be used for health and fitness purposes and integrate with the Health app." — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).
- Guideline 5.1.3(ii): "Apps must not write false or inaccurate data into HealthKit…" — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).
- Apple DTS (Argun Tekant): "Any background capability you might want to employ, similar to Location Updates (Audio, Workouts, Extended Runtime), can only be used for their intended purposes, and not to have apps to execute arbitrary functions in the background… any background capability you use for this without implementing a related feature will carry a risk of your app being rejected." — [Apple Forums 765258](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/765258).
- Apple DTS (Ziqiao Chen), asked about a lone-worker safety app with no fitness features: "No, that is not a valid use case to use HKWorkoutSession, according to App Review Guidelines 2.5.1" — [Apple Forums 778920](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/778920).
- A lucid-dream app was rejected for using HKWorkoutSession without reading fitness data — [Apple Forums 734323](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/734323) (developer report). Apple DevRel advised asking App Review directly about borderline cases — [Apple Forums 773305](https://origin-devforums.apple.com/forums/thread/773305).

**Force-quit**
- No Apple source covers what happens to an iPhone workout session after a force-quit. The documented recovery path covers crashes only ("relaunch your app in case of a crash") — [WWDC25 322](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/322/). The third-party PR explicitly does not handle resuming after the app is killed — [PR #278](https://github.com/TheRealestNwah/fitness-app/pull/278).

### Inferences
- **Background runtime while locked:** Strongly implied, though Apple does not state it in one sentence. Locked-device workout data access, crash relaunch, Siri control from the Lock Screen, and the sample's local Live Activity updates during a locked workout all need the process alive. Apple's sample achieves this with no `workout-processing` mode, so on iOS an active session seems to grant runtime by itself. Verify on a device that the process stays alive for 30–90 minutes while locked.
- **Live steps:** The safest design runs `CMPedometer.startUpdates` inside the app while the session keeps it unsuspended. That gives near-real-time cumulative steps, so a local notification could fire within seconds of crossing the goal. A second option is `enableCollection(for: stepCount)` on the builder, but how fast iPhone step samples reach HealthKit is undocumented and may be batched. The goal is a *daily* step total, so the app needs today's steps before the walk began plus the live delta from the session. Under the project's source-deduplication rules, a Garmin or Apple Watch source could change the "official" total.
- **Latency:** Seconds, limited by the CMPedometer delivery cadence, which is undocumented. This is unverified until measured on a device.
- **User action:** The user taps "Walk to my goal", or asks Siri on the Lock Screen through `INStartWorkoutIntent`. On the first run the system shows its locked-data-access prompt. HealthKit authorization covers the workout type plus the read types.
- **Entitlements:** `com.apple.developer.healthkit`, `NSHealthShareUsageDescription`/`NSHealthUpdateUsageDescription`, `NSSupportsLiveActivities` (optional), Siri entitlement (optional), and `NSMotionUsageDescription` for CMPedometer (standard Core Motion requirement; not fetched in this research). `workout-processing` does not appear to be required on iOS — conflicting evidence above.
- **iOS version:** 26+ only. iOS 16–25 users cannot use this path without an Apple Watch.
- **Battery:** No Apple figures. A walking session without GPS should cost less than an outdoor run with route tracking, but this is unmeasured.
- **App Review:** A "walk to my goal" button that starts a real walking workout fits the intended purpose. It is user-started, saved to Health as an `HKWorkout`, shown on the Lock Screen, and ends on goal or by the user. That plausibly passes. The rejection risk rises if the session starts automatically, runs all day, or saves no workout.
- **Force-quit:** Probably ends the session and the live counting. The user would lose the goal notification until they reopen the app.
- The iOS 26.4+ `finishWorkout` nil/nil reports matter mainly for saving the workout. They do not block live counting, but a robust implementation should handle a failed save.

### Gaps
- No Apple statement explicitly confirms that an iPhone app keeps unlimited background CPU time during an active session, or how the system treats high CPU use on iOS. The watchOS doc warns only that "If your app uses an excessive amount of CPU while in the background, watchOS may suspend it" ([Running workout sessions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/running-workout-sessions)).
- No source confirms whether `HKLiveWorkoutBuilder` on iPhone produces live `stepCount` statistics by default, or how often iPhone pedometer samples reach HealthKit during a session.
- No source covers whether CMPedometer live updates continue while the iPhone is locked during a workout session. This is expected but undocumented.
- No source covers a system status indicator, a maximum session length on iPhone, battery cost, or force-quit behaviour.
- No published App Review decision exists for a step-goal walking session on iPhone.

## 2. BGContinuedProcessingTask (iOS 26): qualifying tasks, user action, progress, UI, limits, fit for a 30–90 minute walk, App Review

### Takeaway
`BGContinuedProcessingTask` (iOS 26+) lets work that the user started from an explicit tap continue in the background, with a system Live Activity showing progress and a Stop button. Apple frames it for finite processing jobs such as exports, uploads, and accessory updates. It expires tasks whose progress stalls, has no guaranteed duration, and has documented problems on a truly locked device. A walk whose progress depends on the user walking is a poor fit and likely to be expired or cancelled.

### Cited Findings
- Availability: iOS 26.0, iPadOS 26.0, Mac Catalyst 26.0. "A task that starts in the foreground and can continue running in the background as needed." — [Apple: BGContinuedProcessingTask](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/bgcontinuedprocessingtask).
- User initiation: "Unlike other BGTask subclasses, BGContinuedProcessingTask starts in the foreground. In addition, your app needs to run the task only in response to someone's action, such as tapping a button." — [Apple: Performing long-running tasks on iOS and iPadOS](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/performing-long-running-tasks-on-ios-and-ipados).
- Apple's examples are video and audio export, thumbnail creation, and image filters or compression. Tasks can use the network, intensive CPU, and the GPU with an entitlement — [Apple: Performing long-running tasks](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/performing-long-running-tasks-on-ios-and-ipados).
- WWDC25 session 227: tasks "always start with an explicit action that someone performs in your app, like a button tap or gesture", have "a clear, immediate goal" such as "exporting file, publishing social media content, or completing an update on a connected accessory", and "make measurable progress". "Avoid automatic workloads like maintenance, backups, or photo syncing. Doing this unexpected work may lead to your app's task being canceled." Work that "requires consent or input" needs "a different approach" — [WWDC25 227 "Finish tasks in the background"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/227/).
- Progress: "If that progression is slower than expected, the system will prompt the initiator, asking if they want the work to continue." "Tasks that do not report any progress will be expired." — [WWDC25 227](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/227/). "The system prioritizes the termination of tasks that reflect minimal or no progress, when resources become constrained." — [Apple: BGContinuedProcessingTask](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/bgcontinuedprocessingtask).
- Stall threshold (informal): Apple DTS Kevin Elliott said "We haven't published an actual number, but I believe it's ~30s", and "progress reporting here is also about telling the system you're still active, not just updating the interface." — [Apple Forums 805554](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/805554).
- System UI: "The system displays the progress of this task in a Live Activity and a person can cancel it through the interface" — [Apple: BGContinuedProcessingTask](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/bgcontinuedprocessingtask). The request's title and subtitle are what the user sees; `updateTitle(_:subtitle:)` changes them — [WWDC25 227](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/227/).
- Time limits: none documented. Apple DTS: "We can't guarantee any specific time, but there isn't any artificial throttle on timing and, theoretically, a task could run for an hour+ under the right circumstances." — [Apple Forums 804919](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/804919). "The system can terminate a continuous background task abruptly depending on run-time conditions" — [Apple: BGContinuedProcessingTask](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/bgcontinuedprocessingtask).
- Submission strategy: the default `queue` runs the task when room is available. `fail` "cancels a fail task right away if it can't begin processing the task immediately, for example, when the system reaches a maximum number of concurrent tasks." — [Apple: Performing long-running tasks](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/performing-long-running-tasks-on-ios-and-ipados).
- Setup: identifiers go in the Info.plist permitted-identifiers list, prefixed with the bundle ID, with new wildcard suffixes (`.*`). Handlers are registered dynamically when the user acts, not at launch — [WWDC25 227](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/227/). The GPU needs `com.apple.developer.background-tasks.continued-processing.gpu` — [Apple: Performing long-running tasks](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/performing-long-running-tasks-on-ios-and-ipados).
- QoS: "iOS prioritizes the foreground experience, meaning your background task may receive a lower quality of service" — [WWDC25 227](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/227/).
- Locked device:
  - Apple DTS confirmed a bug: "The API was intended to keep the device awake, but an oversight meant that… [it] left off the assertion exception that prevented sleep… the behavior here isn't directly tied to the device locking." The issue was filed as FB19916760, which DTS later said "should have been fixed in [iOS 26.1]".
  - A developer still reported the task expiring "only… with the screen truly locked" on iOS 26.1 and 26.2 beta 3 (FB21233240).
  - [Apple Forums 796138](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/796138). In an earlier thread, DTS also said "I think we may expire tasks on device lock" (unconfirmed) — [Apple Forums 804919](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/804919).
- Force-quit: "The system cancels any running tasks if a person closes the app in the app switcher, but the app doesn't receive an indication of cancellation in that case." — [Apple: Performing long-running tasks](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/performing-long-running-tasks-on-ios-and-ipados).
- The expiration handler cannot tell user Stop, timeout, and resource pressure apart. DTS: "this is definitely an issue with the API. Please file an enhancement request." — [Apple Forums 818873](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/818873) (as summarised in search results; see also [805554](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/805554)).

### Inferences
- **Background runtime while locked:** Intended, but unreliable in practice on a truly locked device through at least iOS 26.2 beta, per developer reports. Behaviour on 26.4+ and 27 was not found.
- **Fit for a walk:** Weak. Progress (steps toward the goal) advances only while the user walks, so stops at crossings, rest, or a coffee would stall progress beyond the ~30 s threshold. The system would then prompt "continue?" or expire the task. A walk is also not "processing" work in Apple's framing, which is exports, uploads, and accessory updates. A 30–90 minute walk sits at the very edge of the "hour+ under the right circumstances" DTS comment.
- **Latency:** If the task stays alive, CMPedometer updates would flow (the app is not suspended), so latency could be seconds. Reliability is the problem, not latency.
- **User action:** An explicit in-app tap. The system Live Activity appears automatically and can be cancelled by the user.
- **Entitlements:** No background mode is documented as required beyond the permitted identifier. GPU needs an entitlement, which this feature does not need.
- **iOS version:** 26+.
- **Battery:** Not documented. Apple asks tasks to be "Efficient, Minimal, Resilient, Courteous, and Adaptive" — [WWDC25 227](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/227/).
- **App Review:** No published stance specific to this API. Using it to keep the app alive for pedometer monitoring arguably conflicts with 2.5.4's "intended purposes" and Apple's "clear, immediate goal… measurable progress" framing. Risk is higher than with a genuine workout session.
- **Force-quit:** The task is cancelled, as documented.

### Gaps
- No Apple statement on whether BGContinuedProcessingTask is acceptable for sensor monitoring or user-paced activities.
- No confirmed status of the locked-device expiry on iOS 26.4+ or iOS 27.
- No source confirms whether a `UIBackgroundModes` entry (for example `processing`) is required for BGContinuedProcessingTask.

## 3. Live Activities and Dynamic Island: code execution, push updates, frequent-update budget, alerts, push-to-start, background runtime

### Takeaway
A Live Activity is a display surface rendered by the widget extension. It cannot access the network or location, and it gives the app no ongoing background runtime. Its content changes only when the running app updates it or the server sends an ActivityKit push. That push is subject to an hourly budget, which `NSSupportsLiveActivitiesFrequentUpdates` raises. An update can carry an alert that lights the screen and expands the Dynamic Island. A Live Activity is therefore useful for *showing* progress and *alerting* at the goal, but something else must count the steps.

### Cited Findings
- "Each Live Activity runs in its own sandbox, and, unlike a widget, a Live Activity can't access the network or receive location updates. To update the dynamic data of an active Live Activity, use ActivityKit in your app or allow your Live Activities to receive ActivityKit push notifications." "The code that describes the user interface of your Live Activity is part of your app's widget extension." — [Apple: Displaying live data with Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities).
- Duration: "A Live Activity can be active for up to eight hours… the Live Activity remains on the Lock Screen… for up to four additional hours… a maximum of 12 hours." Data limit: "can't exceed a combined size of 4 KB." — [Apple: Displaying live data](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities).
- Starting and updating: "In general, your app needs to be in the foreground to start a Live Activity. You can update or end a Live Activity from your app while it runs in the background — for example, by using BackgroundTasks. However, you can start a Live Activity while your app is in the background by using an app intent that conforms to LiveActivityIntent." — [Apple: Displaying live data](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities).
- Alerts: "use the update(_:alertConfiguration:) function to display an alert on iPhone, iPad, and a paired Apple Watch… On Apple Watch, the system uses the title and body attributes for the alert. On iPhone and iPad, the system doesn't show a regular alert but instead shows the expanded Live Activity in the Dynamic Island or the Lock Screen presentation as a banner on devices without the Dynamic Island." — [Apple: Displaying live data](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities). A push payload can include an `alert` (title, body, sound) "to light up their device and display the expanded presentation" — [Apple: Starting and updating Live Activities with ActivityKit push notifications](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/starting-and-updating-live-activities-with-activitykit-push-notifications).
- Budget: "The system allows for a certain budget of ActivityKit push notifications per hour." Priority 10 (the default) counts toward the budget, and priority 5 does not. "If you exceed the budget, the system may throttle your ActivityKit push notifications." — [Apple: ActivityKit push notifications](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/starting-and-updating-live-activities-with-activitykit-push-notifications). Apple publishes no number.
- Frequent updates: add `NSSupportsLiveActivitiesFrequentUpdates` = YES. "People can deactivate frequent ActivityKit push notifications for your app in the Settings app", which apps can detect with `frequentPushesEnabled` — [Apple: ActivityKit push notifications](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/starting-and-updating-live-activities-with-activitykit-push-notifications).
- Apple Frameworks Engineer: "APNs with frequent updates enabled is the supported way to update a Live Activity that needs a lot of updates. Other background types can allow for more frequent updates, but this behavior is not explicitly supported." "We purposely discourage and throttle update frequencies similar to a now playing experience… usually priority and budget matter the most here… your LA is actually tied to the system (Lock Screen, Springboard) so those processes also have a say in whether an update takes place." — [Apple Forums 748569](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/748569).
- Push-to-start: "On devices that run iOS or iPadOS 17.1 and earlier, you can't start Live Activities with ActivityKit push notifications", so it needs iOS 17.2+. It uses `Activity.pushToStartTokenUpdates`, and start payloads require an `alert`. "When the system receives the ActivityKit push notification on a device, it starts a new Live Activity, wakes up your app, and grants it background runtime to allow you to download assets…" For updates, "the system wakes the widget extension to render the UI". iOS 18 adds broadcast channel starts — [Apple: ActivityKit push notifications](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/starting-and-updating-live-activities-with-activitykit-push-notifications).
- Apple's iOS 26 workout sample updates its Live Activity locally from the app during the workout (`staleDate: Date.now + 15`) — [Apple sample](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/building-a-workout-app-for-iphone-and-ipad).
- Force-quit (anecdotal): developers report that after a force-quit, push-to-start still shows the Live Activity, but `pushTokenUpdates` never fires, so the server cannot send updates. Updates continue if the app has been launched at least once since — [Apple Forums 834934](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/834934); [Apple Forums 834923](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/834923); [Apple Forums 792955](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/792955).
- Guideline 4.5.3 forbids using Live Activities to "spam, phish, or send unsolicited messages" — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).
- Availability: Live Activities came with iOS 16.1, but the fetched pages do not state the version. The push-to-start lower bound of 17.2 is documented as above.

### Inferences
- **Background runtime:** None of its own. Push-to-start gives a brief asset-download wake, and token refresh gives brief runtime. The widget extension only renders, and cannot run Core Motion or poll steps. "Can't access the network or receive location updates" is documented; Core Motion is not mentioned, but the extension has no execution model in which to poll it.
- **Server-push path latency:** Bounded by how fast step data reaches the server. That is the HealthKit background delivery, `stepCount` hourly maximum on iOS, covered in §5 and probably by other researchers. A server push therefore cannot deliver "within seconds" unless the phone is already running and uploading.
- **Best use:** Pair a Live Activity with an HKWorkoutSession (§1). The running app updates progress locally and, at the goal, calls `update(_:alertConfiguration:)` and/or posts a local notification. The alert expands the Dynamic Island, shows a banner on non-island devices, and shows a title/body alert on a paired Apple Watch.
- **Entitlements:** `NSSupportsLiveActivities`, optionally `NSSupportsLiveActivitiesFrequentUpdates`, and the APNs push capability for remote updates.
- **App Review:** Low risk for progress display and a goal alert.

### Gaps
- No published numbers for the hourly push budget, with or without frequent updates.
- No official statement on Live Activity push updates after a force-quit (anecdotes only).
- No source confirms whether local `Activity.update` calls from a background-running workout app are throttled while locked. The Frameworks Engineer comment suggests the system can refuse updates.

## 4. App Intents / Shortcuts personal automations: background triggers, CMPedometer plus notification, step or activity triggers

### Takeaway
Shortcuts personal automations can run an app intent without confirmation for many triggers, including time of day, arrive/leave, app open, Wi-Fi, and Apple Watch Workout start/end. No step-count, activity, or Health-data trigger exists. An intent run in the background gets about 30 seconds. iOS 27's `LongRunningIntent` extends that with mandatory progress reporting and a Live Activity, much like BGContinuedProcessingTask. Automations can do one-shot checks, but cannot provide live monitoring until the goal is reached.

### Cited Findings
- Event triggers include Time of Day, Alarm, Sleep, Keyboard, Screenshot, **Notification (filtered by app, with message/title filters)**, **Apple Watch Workout (by workout type, at start, end, or both)**, and Sound Recognition. No trigger is based on steps, activity, or Health data — [Apple Support: Event triggers in Shortcuts](https://support.apple.com/guide/shortcuts/event-triggers-apd932ff833f/ios).
- "Some personal automations can run without asking you for confirmation when they're triggered." These include "Time of Day, Alarm, Sleep, Arrive, Leave, CarPlay, Email, Message, Transaction, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Apple Watch Workout, NFC, App, Airplane Mode, Do Not Disturb, Low Power Mode, Battery Level, Charger, Sound Recognition." An "Allow Running When Locked" privacy setting exists. "You also may need to set individual actions to run automatically." — [Apple Support: Add automations](https://support.apple.com/guide/shortcuts/add-automations-apdfbdbd7123/ios).
- 30-second limit: "When a task runs in the background, the system traditionally gives it up to 30 seconds… In iOS, iPadOS, tvOS, visionOS, and watchOS, the system gives tasks 30 seconds to run in the background unless you use the methods of this protocol to extend that runtime." — [Apple: LongRunningIntent (iOS 27.0+)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appintents/longrunningintent).
- `LongRunningIntent` (iOS/iPadOS/watchOS 27.0+) inherits `ProgressReportingIntent`: "If you don't update this property regularly, the system can cancel the background runtime extension and end your task prematurely." Apple's examples are file operations, data sync, ML inference, and data processing — [Apple: LongRunningIntent](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appintents/longrunningintent). WWDC26 "Discover new capabilities in the App Intents framework", quoted by a blogger: "When your intent runs – from Siri, Shortcuts, or any system surface – it only has 30 seconds to finish." "progress updates appear automatically as a Live Activity", with a stop button — [Matthew Cassinelli](https://matthewcassinelli.com/app-intents-thirty-second-limit-extend-execution-live-activity-longrunningintent/) (secondary transcript quote).
- `LiveActivityIntent` can start a Live Activity while the app is in the background — [Apple: Displaying live data](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities).
- Developers report timeouts ("didn't finish executing in time") for long intents that do not open the app — [Apple Forums WWDC22-10170 tag](https://origin-devforums.apple.com/forums/tags/wwdc2022-10170) (anecdotal).
- Lock Screen Siri workout intents (`INStartWorkoutIntent`, and so on) can start an iPhone workout session without unlocking (iOS 26) — [WWDC25 322](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/322/); [Apple sample](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/building-a-workout-app-for-iphone-and-ipad).

### Inferences
- **Reading steps and notifying:** An intent run by automation executes in the app's process (or its extension) for up to ~30 s. Within that window it could plausibly run a one-shot `CMPedometer.queryPedometerData` or a HealthKit query, compare against the goal, and post a local notification. This is standard API use, but no Apple page specifically confirms CMPedometer in an automation-run intent, and HealthKit data is protected while the device is locked.
- **Semi-passive use:** Time-of-day automations (for example hourly from 5–9 pm) could "nudge-check" progress, but that is minutes-to-hours latency, not seconds. Users must create the automation themselves; apps can suggest App Shortcuts but cannot create personal automations programmatically (not verified in this research).
- **"Apple Watch Workout" trigger:** When the user starts an Apple Watch Walk workout, an iPhone automation can run immediately. It could start the app's own iPhone tracking, for example an intent that begins an `HKWorkoutSession` on iOS 26, or a Live Activity via `LiveActivityIntent`. That makes it a semi-passive starter, but it requires an Apple Watch.
- **"Notification" trigger:** In theory this could react to another app's notification. It is fragile and requires user setup. No evidence was found that Apple's Fitness app posts a step-goal notification; see §6.
- **`LongRunningIntent` (iOS 27):** Same poor fit as BGContinuedProcessingTask for a user-paced walk, because progress must keep advancing.
- **Force-quit:** Automations launch the intent regardless of a prior force-quit. Not verified in this research.
- **App Review:** Low risk for App Shortcuts and automations. 2.5.4 still applies to any background runtime used.

### Gaps
- No Apple documentation explicitly confirms that a background-run intent may use CMPedometer and HealthKit while locked, or whether "Allow Running When Locked" changes data-protection access.
- The exact `openAppWhenRun`/`supportedModes` behaviour in iOS 26/27 automations was not researched in depth.

## 5. Apple Watch companion: watchOS workout session, background delivery, extended runtime, complications, latency, user coverage

### Takeaway
On Apple Watch, an `HKWorkoutSession` with the `workout-processing` background mode keeps the app running for the whole session, even with the wrist down. The Watch app can therefore count steps live and alert on the wrist within seconds, and it can mirror the session to the iPhone (iOS 17+), which launches the iPhone app in the background. Without a workout, watchOS background delivery is limited to about four wakes per hour, and only with a complication on the active face. A Watch companion is the strongest live-alert path, but it helps only Apple Watch owners. Survey estimates of that share conflict.

### Cited Findings
- "Apps with an active workout session can run in the background… Workout sessions require the Workout processing background mode." "Your app continues to run throughout the entire workout session, even when the user lowers their wrist or interacts with a different app." "Your app continues to receive data from HealthKit and Apple Watch's sensors in the background." "If your app uses an excessive amount of CPU while in the background, watchOS may suspend it." — [Apple: Running workout sessions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/running-workout-sessions).
- `workout-processing` is listed as a watchOS-only background mode — [Apple: Configuring background execution modes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/configuring-background-execution-modes).
- Companion guidance: "If the user doesn't start and stop a workout session in your watchOS app, don't try to retroactively create a workout session on Apple Watch." — [Apple: Running workout sessions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/running-workout-sessions).
- Mirroring (WWDC23):
  - "When a mirrored session starts on my Apple Watch, if my iPhone app is not running, it will be launched in the background and handed over the workout session."
  - "HealthKit launches my companion iPhone app in the background, gives my app 10 seconds to start a live activity and call a handler to start mirroring."
  - Data flows both ways via `sendData(toRemoteWorkoutSession:)`.
  - The iPhone can launch the Watch app with the "existing Start Watch App API".
  - [WWDC23 10023 "Build a multi-device workout app"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10023/). WWDC25 says: "If you have a Watch app, be sure to start the workout there to get all available metrics… And once you have, be sure to mirror the workout to the iPhone." — [WWDC25 322](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/322/).
- Mirroring pitfalls (anecdotal): a "Remote session delegate is not set up" error appears if data is sent too early — [nonstrict.eu](https://nonstrict.eu/blog/2024/hkworkoutsession-remote-delegate-not-setup-error) — and a "Remote device is unreachable" error has been reported — [Apple Forums 751232](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/751232).
- Background delivery:
  - "In watchOS, most data types have an hourly maximum frequency." "In watchOS, the background updates share a budget with WKApplicationRefreshBackgroundTask tasks. Your app can receive four updates (or background app refresh tasks) an hour, as long as it has a complication on the active watch face."
  - "On iOS, stepCount samples have an hourly maximum frequency."
  - Background delivery requires `com.apple.developer.healthkit.background-delivery` (iOS 15/watchOS 8+).
  - [Apple: enableBackgroundDelivery](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/enablebackgrounddelivery(for:frequency:withcompletion:)).
- `WKExtendedRuntimeSession` types: Self care (10 min, frontmost), Mindfulness (1 h, frontmost), Physical therapy (1 h, background), Smart alarm (30 min, background, schedulable). "Each app can only support one type of extended runtime session." Physical therapy: "If the physical therapy activity is strenuous—for example, riding an exercise bike—consider using an HKWorkoutSession instead." — [Apple: Using extended runtime sessions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/using-extended-runtime-sessions).
- App Review: Apple DTS lists "Workouts, Extended Runtime" among capabilities that "can only be used for their intended purposes" — [Apple Forums 765258](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/765258).
- Shortcuts "Apple Watch Workout" trigger on iPhone (start/end, by type), runnable without confirmation — [Apple Support: Event triggers](https://support.apple.com/guide/shortcuts/event-triggers-apd932ff833f/ios); [Apple Support: Add automations](https://support.apple.com/guide/shortcuts/add-automations-apdfbdbd7123/ios).
- User coverage:
  - CIRP: among US customers who bought an iPhone in the 12 months ending June 2024, "about 60% also own an Apple Watch" — [AppleWorld.Today on CIRP](https://appleworld.today/2024/09/apples-recent-apple-watch-and-airpods-announcements-are-important-of-iphone-buyers).
  - Other headlines report "35% of iPhone users have a smartwatch" — [iMore](https://www.imore.com/35-iphone-users-have-smartwatch-compared-just-16-android-users) (undated in results).
  - "Only 12% own the Apple trio" (iPhone, Watch, and AirPods) — [PhoneArena](https://phonearena.com/news/iPhone-Apple-Watch-and-AirPods-walk-into-a-research-Results-Only-12-own-the-Apple-trio_id151285).
  - Estimates conflict depending on sample and definition.

### Inferences
- **Watch workout latency:** Seconds. The Watch app runs throughout, can read `CMPedometer` or builder statistics live, and can post a local notification or haptic on the wrist. It can also `sendData` to the mirrored iPhone session to update the iPhone Live Activity or notify there.
- **User action:** Start a walk in the Watch app, or tap on iPhone, which uses `startWatchApp` to launch the Watch app with the configuration. Needs watchOS app development and the `workout-processing` mode on the Watch target.
- **Watch without a workout:** Four wakes per hour with an active complication means ≥15 min latency. That is not "within seconds".
- **The client's tester uses iPhone + Garmin, not an Apple Watch** (project context). A Watch companion would not help that user. Garmin step data reaches HealthKit only via Garmin Connect sync, outside this research's scope.
- **App Review:** A genuine Watch walking workout is the canonical intended use.
- **Force-quit:** Force-quitting the iPhone app does not stop the Watch session (inference). Background launch for mirroring is documented, but its interaction with an iPhone force-quit is undocumented.
- **iOS version:** Mirroring needs iOS 17+ / watchOS 10+ (mirroring APIs from WWDC23). The Watch workout session itself has been available since watchOS 2–5.

### Gaps
- No measured latency for Watch-to-iPhone `sendData` or mirrored-session updates.
- No authoritative 2025–2026 figure for the share of iPhone users who own an Apple Watch, and no figure for this app's user base.
- No source checked whether watchOS HKLiveWorkoutBuilder provides live `stepCount` for walking workouts. CMPedometer on the Watch is the likely source (not verified).
- Battery cost of a 30–90 minute Watch walking workout was not found from an Apple source. A blog's "6–8 hours" figure for continuous Apple Watch workouts is uncited ([blakecrosley.com](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/watchos-workout-lifecycle)).

## 6. Apple-provided activity notifications (Move goal, Fitness) that third parties could rely on

### Takeaway
No public API was found that lets a third-party app subscribe to, trigger, or customise Apple's own Activity or Fitness goal notifications. Apps can only read activity summaries and draw rings, then send their own alerts. Apple's rings are calorie, exercise-minute, and stand based, not a step goal. The only "hook" is the Shortcuts "Notification" trigger, which is an indirect, user-configured workaround with no evidence that it works for Fitness.

### Cited Findings
- HealthKit exposes activity summary data for reading via `HKActivitySummaryQuery` and display via `HKActivityRingView` — [Apple: HKActivityRingView](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkitui/hkactivityringview). The watchOS sample reads `activitySummaryType()` and `activityMoveMode` — [Apple: Running workout sessions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/running-workout-sessions).
- Third-party ring apps such as "All the Rings" send their *own* goal alerts from HealthKit reads — [App Store: All the Rings](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1186956966).
- An unanswered developer forum question asks whether third parties can show Fitness awards — [Apple Forums 720856](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/720856).
- Without an Apple Watch, the iPhone Fitness app (iOS 16+) shows only the Move ring, estimated from iPhone motion data and third-party workouts — [iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2022/06/06/ios-16-to-bring-the-fitness-app-to-all-iphones-no-apple-watch-needed/); [iGeeksBlog](https://www.igeeksblog.com/how-to-use-fitness-app-on-iphone/) (secondary).
- Shortcuts has a "Notification" event trigger filterable by app and text, but no Health or activity trigger — [Apple Support: Event triggers](https://support.apple.com/guide/shortcuts/event-triggers-apd932ff833f/ios).
- Workouts saved by third-party apps feed Apple's rings, so an `HKWorkout` from §1 counts toward Move and Exercise — [Apple: Using extended runtime sessions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/using-extended-runtime-sessions) (cross-training credit note); [iGeeksBlog](https://www.igeeksblog.com/how-to-use-fitness-app-on-iphone/).

### Inferences
- Third parties cannot rely on Apple's notifications for a *step* goal. Apple has no user-configurable step-goal notification to piggy-back on (not verified exhaustively), and no API exposes Fitness notification events.
- The Shortcuts Notification-trigger workaround needs the user to build an automation. It depends on Fitness posting a notification, and it measures calories, not steps. It is not product-grade.

### Gaps
- No Apple document explicitly states "third parties cannot observe Fitness notifications". The conclusion rests on the absence of any API in HealthKit and Shortcuts documentation.
- Whether the iPhone-only Fitness app posts Move-goal-completion notifications was not confirmed.

## 7. Cross-mechanism comparison (summary of the objective's attributes)

### Takeaway
For "tap a button, walk, get notified within seconds of the step goal while locked", only two paths are documented to keep the app running for the whole walk: **iOS 26 iPhone HKWorkoutSession** and an **Apple Watch HKWorkoutSession**. Both pair well with a Live Activity alert. BGContinuedProcessingTask and iOS 27 `LongRunningIntent` require continuously advancing progress and are framed for processing jobs. Live Activities and automations provide no sustained runtime of their own.

### Cited Findings
| Mechanism | Runtime while locked | Achievable latency | User action | Entitlements / modes | iOS | App Review fit for "walk to goal" | Force-quit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| iPhone `HKWorkoutSession` | Designed for locked use: locked-data prompt, crash relaunch, sample updates Live Activity ([WWDC25 322](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/322/); [sample](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/building-a-workout-app-for-iphone-and-ipad)) | Seconds (CMPedometer in a live process; undocumented cadence) | Tap or Lock Screen Siri; first-run system prompt | HealthKit; sample declares only `processing` mode; `workout-processing` listed watchOS-only ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/configuring-background-execution-modes)) | 26+ | Plausible if a genuine workout; non-fitness use rejected ([778920](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/778920)) | Undocumented; crash recovery only |
| `BGContinuedProcessingTask` | Intended but buggy on a truly locked device, 26.0–26.2b ([796138](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/796138)) | Seconds if alive | Explicit in-app tap ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/performing-long-running-tasks-on-ios-and-ipados)) | Permitted identifier; GPU entitlement optional | 26+ | Weak: processing-job framing; stalls expire (~30 s) ([805554](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/805554)) | Cancelled, no callback ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/performing-long-running-tasks-on-ios-and-ipados)) |
| Live Activity (+ APNs) | No runtime; extension renders only ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities)) | Bound by data source; push budget per hour ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/starting-and-updating-live-activities-with-activitykit-push-notifications)) | Start in app, by LiveActivityIntent, or push-to-start (17.2+) | `NSSupportsLiveActivities`, optional `…FrequentUpdates`, APNs | 16.1+ (push-to-start 17.2+) | Fine (4.5.3 no spam) | Anecdotally loses update token if never relaunched ([834934](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/834934)) |
| App Intent / Shortcuts automation | ~30 s per run ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appintents/longrunningintent)) | Trigger-bound (time, place, Watch workout); no step trigger ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/shortcuts/event-triggers-apd932ff833f/ios)) | User must build the automation | None special | 16+ (run-immediately list per current guide); `LongRunningIntent` 27+ | Fine | Not researched |
| Watch `HKWorkoutSession` (+ mirroring) | Whole session, wrist down ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/running-workout-sessions)) | Seconds on wrist; iPhone launched in background via mirroring ([WWDC23](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10023/)) | Start on Watch, or iPhone via startWatchApp | `workout-processing` (watchOS), HealthKit | watchOS; mirroring iOS 17+ | Canonical intended use | iPhone force-quit does not stop the Watch session (inference) |
| watchOS background delivery (no workout) | 4 wakes per hour with complication ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/enablebackgrounddelivery(for:frequency:withcompletion:))) | ≥15 min | None after setup | background-delivery entitlement | watchOS 8+ for entitlement | Fine | n/a |

### Inferences
- **Recommended path (iOS 26+):** A "Walk to my goal" button starts a walking `HKWorkoutSession`. The app runs `CMPedometer.startUpdates` from start of day (or the pre-walk total plus the live delta), updates a Live Activity, and at the goal posts a local notification and/or `update(_:alertConfiguration:)`. It then offers to end and save the workout.
- **Fallback for iOS 16–25 without a Watch:** No documented user-initiated mechanism keeps the app running while locked. Alternatives such as location background mode or audio are outside this note's scope and carry 2.5.4 risk.
- **Watch owners:** A watchOS companion with workout mirroring gives the most reliable second-level alert. It does not help Garmin-only users such as the client tester.
- All latency figures above are design estimates. A physical-device test while locked (30–90 minute walk, iOS 26.x/27) is required. A simulator build cannot validate it, per project guidance.

### Gaps
- No Apple-published battery figures for any of these mechanisms. Apple only warns that background-mode "overuse can negatively impact device performance and battery life" ([Apple: Configuring background execution modes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/configuring-background-execution-modes)).
- No developer field report was found that measures end-to-end step-goal notification latency with an iOS 26 iPhone workout session while locked.
